📗 Modals of Advice: Should, Ought to, Had Better

English — Chapter 1 Complete Grammar Notes

📖 Overview & Introduction

Modal verbs are essential in English for expressing various ideas. The three modal verbs we focus on here are:

  • Should
  • Ought to
  • Had better

These modals are crucial because your exam is concerned with these three! 🎯

❓ What are modal verbs?

Modal verbs are auxiliary/helping verbs that help us express ideas such as:

  • 🔹 Ability
  • 💡 Advice
  • 📌 Obligation

Many modal verbs have more than one meaning. They are always followed by the base (simple) form of a verb.

Example: Amanda should go to the doctor.

→ This sentence suggests that we think it is a good idea for Amanda to visit the doctor.

💡 Modals for Advice

English speakers use the modal verbs “should,” “ought to,” and “had better” to express that they think something is a good (or a bad) idea.

📌 Should is the most common way to give advice in everyday English.

📊 Usage Patterns & Examples

Form Modal / Example
Affirmative • A: I failed my test.
B: Really? You should study harder.

• A: It's really cold outside.
B: You ought to wear a warm jacket.

• A: You had better slow down. You are driving too fast!
Negative • Young children shouldn't watch violent TV shows.

• You ought not to watch TV before finishing your homework.
(Note: “Ought to” is rarely used in the negative form)

• You had better not forget to pay your tuition. If you do, the university will kick you out!
Question • A: I have a problem. Should I call my parents or my friend?

(Note: “Ought to” and “Had better” are not common in question forms)

⚠️ Negative Forms & Contractions

There is a distinct difference in how negative forms and contractions are made:

  • You can make a contraction only with the verb should (shouldn't).
  • The word not goes directly after should and had better, but in the middle of ought to (ought not to).
Negative Sentence Contraction Status
You should not put hot water on a burn. You shouldn't put hot water on a burn. ✓
You ought not to put hot water on a burn. No contraction available.
You had better not put hot water on a burn. No contraction available.

🔥 Understanding "Had Better" (Strength & Warning)

While all three modals share the same basic advice message, “had better” is stronger. It includes the idea of a warning: something bad will happen if you do not follow the advice.

💡 Examples of Warnings:

  • You had better not forget to pay your tuition. If you forget, the university will kick you out!
  • You had better do your homework. If you do not do your homework, the teacher will give you a low mark.

📝 Speaking Note: “You had better...” can be contracted to “You'd better...” in common speech.

⚠️ Some native speakers say, “You better ...”, but this is grammatically incorrect. The word “had” is necessary in standard English.

✳️ Sentence Structure Rules

Modals are followed directly by the simple (base) form of a verb:

Pattern: [Subject] + [modal] + [basic verb] + ...
Correct: You should study harder.

Common Grammatical Errors to Avoid:

  • You should to study harder. ❌ Wrong!
  • You had better slowing down. ❌ Wrong!
  • She ought to to drink more water. ❌ Wrong!

📌 Remember that in “ought to,” the “to” is part of the modal verb itself and is not an infinitive “to.”

❓ Question Structure Rules

Pattern: [Modal] + [subject] + [basic verb] + ...?
Example: Should I call my parents or my friend?

WH- (Information) Questions: Place the WH- word immediately before the modal.

  • What should I do about my problem?
  • Where should we have dinner tonight?
  • Why should you believe them?
  • When should they call their boss?

📌 Quick Reference Summary

💡 Should — My Opinion & Personal Advice

  • You should finish your homework before you go out.
  • The builders should have finished by the end of the week.
  • Who should I pass the message to? (in WH- questions)
  • What should I do if I have any problems?
  • I should read the script on the train tomorrow but I know that I'll be too tired. (giving advice to myself)
  • I should wash my hair tonight, I haven't washed it for a week!

📜 Ought to — Obligation & Expectation

  • You ought to finish your homework before you go out. (It is your obligation)
  • The builders ought to have finished by the end of the week. (It is their obligation / contract limit)

🚨 Had better — Urgency, Demand, Threat & Worry

  • There's someone moving about downstairs. We had better call the police, quickly!
  • He had better not be late again or he'll be in trouble.
  • Damn. It is pouring down. We had better get a taxi or we'll get soaked.

🎯 Practice Exercise 1

Complete each sentence with an appropriate modal (should, ought to, had better):

  1. My teacher told me that I should / ought to review my notes every day. I guess it's a good idea to look at them after class.
  2. Do you think Carol should save her money or spend it? She is planning to go to Europe this summer.
  3. I'm going to a party tonight. What should I wear: a dress or black pants?
  4. Our car's gas tank is almost empty. We had better fill it up with gas soon or we will run out of gas!
  5. If you are worried about getting fat, you shouldn't eat at McDonald's every day. You should / ought to eat healthy, low-fat foods like fruits and vegetables.
  6. It's really raining outside right now. I had better not forget my umbrella or I'll get wet!
  7. Where should we have lunch today: at home or in a restaurant?
  8. My doctor feels that I should / ought to exercise more because I'm out of shape and want to lose weight. I think he's right.
