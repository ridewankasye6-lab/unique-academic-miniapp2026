📘 Modals of Suggestion and Advice: Should, Ought to, Had better

💡 Introduction

These three verbs are modal verbs:

  • should
  • ought to
  • had better

Modal verbs are helping/auxiliary verbs that express ideas like ability, advice, and obligation.

Many modal verbs have more than one meaning. They are always followed by the simple form of a verb.

💡 For example: Amanda should go to the doctor.
This shows that we think it is a good idea for Amanda to visit the doctor.

💬 Giving Advice in English

English speakers use the modal verbs “should,” “ought to” and “had better” to express that they think something is a good (or a bad) idea. “Should” is the most common way to give advice.

📊 Look at the following examples:

Form Examples
Affirmative • A: I failed my test.
B: Really? You should study harder.

• A: It's really cold outside.
B: You ought to wear a warm jacket.

• A: You had better slow down. You are driving too fast!
Negative • Young children shouldn't watch violent TV shows.

• You ought not to watch TV before finishing your homework.
(“ought to” is rarely used in the negative form)

• You had better not forget to pay your tuition. If you do, the university will kick you out!
Question • A: I have a problem. Should I call my parents or my friend?

(“ought to” is not common in question form)
(“had better” is not usually used in question form)

⚠️ Negative Forms & Contractions

There is a difference in the negative form as well. You can make a contraction only with the verb should, not the other two. Also, note the position of the word not in negative sentences. The word not goes after should and had better but in the middle of ought to ("ought not to").

Negative Sentence Contraction
You should not put hot water on a burn. You shouldn't put hot water on a burn.
You ought not to put hot water on a burn. (There is no contraction.)
You had better not put hot water on a burn. (There is no contraction.)

🚨 Degree of Strength (Had Better as a Warning)

These examples have the same basic advice message, but “had better” is a bit stronger. It includes the idea of a warning: something bad will happen if you do not follow my advice.

💡 For example:
• You had better not forget to pay your tuition. If you forget, the university will kick you out.
• You had better do your homework. If you do not do your homework, the teacher will give you a low mark.
📌 Note: “You had better...” can be contracted to become “You'd better....” This is correct grammar, and very common in speaking.
Some native speakers say, “You better ...,” but this is incorrect. The “had” is necessary in good grammar.

✳️ Sentence Patterns & Common Errors

In the above examples, you can see that the modals are followed by the simple form of a verb in these patterns:

Subject + modal + basic verb + ...
✓ You should study harder.
  • ❌ You should to study harder. (Wrong!)
  • ❌ You had better slowing down. (Wrong!)
  • ❌ She ought to to drink more water. (Wrong!)

Remember: “ought to” is a modal verb and is followed by a simple verb. The “to” is not an infinitive “to.”

❓ Forming Questions

Modal + subject + basic verb + ...?
Should I call my parents or my friend?

WH- (information) questions can also be formed by putting the WH- question word immediately before the modal.

💡 For example:
• What should I do about my problem?
• Where should we have dinner tonight?
• Why should you believe them?
• When should they call their boss?

📌 Summary

1. Should – my opinion

  • You should finish your homework before you go out.
  • The builders should have finished by the end of the week.

2. Should – in wh-questions

  • Who should I pass the message to?
  • What should I do if I have any problems?

3. Should – giving advice to myself

  • I should read the script on the train tomorrow but I know that I'll be too tired.
  • I should wash my hair tonight, I haven't washed it for a week!

4. Ought to – obligation

  • You ought to finish your homework before you go out. (it is your obligation)
  • The builders ought to have finished by the end of the week. (it is their obligation, contract limit)

5. Had better – urgency, demand, threat, worry

  • There's someone moving about downstairs. We had better call the police, quickly.
  • He had better not be late again or he'll be in trouble.
  • Damn. It is pouring down. We had better get a taxi or we'll get soaked.

🎯 Practice Exercise 1

Complete each sentence with an appropriate modal.

  1. Question 1: My teacher told me that I _________ review my notes every day. I guess it's a good idea to look at them after class.
    ✅ Answer: should / ought to
  2. Question 2: Do you think Carol _________ save her money or spend it? She is planning to go to Europe this summer.
    ✅ Answer: should
  3. Question 3: I'm going to a party tonight. What _________ I wear: a dress or black pants?
    ✅ Answer: should
  4. Question 4: Our car's gas tank is almost empty. We _________ fill it up with gas soon or we will run out of gas!
    ✅ Answer: had better
  5. Question 5: If you are worried about getting fat, you _________ eat at McDonald's every day. You _________ eat healthy, low-fat foods like fruits and vegetables.
    ✅ Answer: shouldn't / had better not | should / ought to
  6. Question 6: It's really raining outside right now. I _________ forget my umbrella or I'll get wet!
    ✅ Answer: had better not
  7. Question 7: Where _________ we have lunch today: at home or in a restaurant?
    ✅ Answer: should
  8. Question 8: My doctor feels that I _________ exercise more because I'm out of shape and want to lose weight. I think he's right.
    ✅ Answer: should / ought to
