🍔 DIGESTIVE SYSTEM

The digestive system is responsible for breaking down food into absorbable molecules, absorbing nutrients, and eliminating indigestible waste. It consists of the alimentary canal (gastrointestinal tract) and accessory organs.

⚙️ Functions of the Digestive System

  • Ingestion: Taking food into the mouth.
  • Propulsion: Movement of food through the tract (swallowing, peristalsis).
  • Mechanical digestion: Physical breakdown (chewing, churning).
  • Chemical digestion: Enzymatic breakdown of macromolecules.
  • Absorption: Passage of digested nutrients into blood/lymph.
  • Defecation: Elimination of indigestible waste.

🧱 Organs of the Digestive System

👄 A. Mouth (Oral Cavity)

Structures:

  • Teeth: Mechanical breakdown (mastication).
  • Tongue: Manipulates food, contains taste buds, aids in swallowing.
  • Salivary glands: Parotid, submandibular, sublingual.
  • Saliva:
    • Composition: 99% water, electrolytes, mucus, enzymes, lysozyme.
    • Functions: Moistens food, begins starch digestion, antibacterial.

🗣️ B. Pharynx and Esophagus

  • Pharynx: Common passage for food and air.
    • Divided into: Nasopharynx, oropharynx, laryngopharynx.
  • Esophagus:
    • Muscular tube (25 cm) connecting pharynx to stomach
    • Passes through esophageal hiatus of diaphragm
    • Contains upper and lower esophageal sphincters
  • Swallowing (Deglutition):
    • Buccal phase: Voluntary - tongue pushes bolus to pharynx.
    • Pharyngeal phase: Involuntary - soft palate elevates, epiglottis covers larynx.
    • Esophageal phase: Peristalsis moves bolus to stomach.

🧪 C. Stomach

Regions:

  • Cardia: Entry area near esophagus.
  • Fundus: Dome-shaped upper portion.
  • Body: Main central region.
  • Pylorus: Lower portion with pyloric sphincter.
⚙️ Functions of the Stomach:
  • Mechanical digestion (churning)
  • Chemical digestion (proteins begin)
  • Storage of food
  • Production of intrinsic factor
  • Limited absorption (water, alcohol, some drugs)

🌀 D. Small Intestine

Length: ~6 meters

Regions: Duodenum (25 cm), Jejunum (~2.5 m), Ileum (~3.5 m)

Structural Adaptations for Absorption:

Structure Description Function
Circular folds (plicae circulares) Permanent transverse folds Increase surface area 3x
Villi Finger-like projections (1 mm tall) Increase surface area 10x
Microvilli Microscopic projections on epithelial cells Increase surface area 20x (brush border)

🪱 E. Large Intestine

Length: ~1.5 meters

Regions: Cecum (with appendix), Colon (ascending, transverse, descending, sigmoid), Rectum, Anal canal.

⚙️ Functions of Large Intestine:
  • Absorption of water and electrolytes
  • Compaction of indigestible material into feces
  • Bacterial fermentation of undigested carbohydrates
  • Vitamin production (Vitamin K, some B vitamins by gut bacteria)

📍 Absorption Sites & Mechanisms

Vitamin B₁₂ B₁₂ + intrinsic factor Ileum Receptor-mediated endocytosis

🗺️ Regional Breakdown of Absorption

  • A. Mouth: Limited absorption: Some drugs (nitroglycerin).
  • B. Stomach: Water (small amounts), Alcohol (ethanol), Aspirin (lipid-soluble form), Some lipid-soluble drugs.
  • C. Small Intestine (Primary absorption site):
    • Duodenum: Iron (Fe²⁺), Calcium (Ca²⁺), Folate, Monosaccharides (glucose, galactose), Amino acids.
    • Jejunum: Most monosaccharides, Most amino acids and dipeptides, Fatty acids and monoglycerides (after micelle formation), Fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K), Water-soluble vitamins (except B₁₂), Electrolytes (Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻).
    • Ileum: Vitamin B₁₂ (requires intrinsic factor), Bile salts (enterohepatic circulation), Remaining nutrients not absorbed earlier.
  • D. Large Intestine: Water (80% of remaining water absorbed), Electrolytes (Na⁺, Cl⁻), Short-chain fatty acids (from bacterial fermentation of fiber), Vitamin K (produced by gut bacteria), Some B vitamins.

🫀 CIRCULATORY SYSTEM

🧩 Components of the Circulatory System

The circulatory system consists of:

  • Heart: Muscular pump.
  • Blood vessels: Arteries, arterioles, capillaries, venules, veins.
  • Blood: Fluid connective tissue.
⚙️ Functions of the Circulatory System:
  • Transport: O₂, CO₂, nutrients, hormones, wastes
  • Regulation: Temperature, pH, fluid balance
  • Protection: Immunity, clotting

🩺 Blood Vessels: Structure and Function

🅰️ A. Arteries

  • Carry blood away from the heart
  • Thick walls with three layers:
    • Tunica intima: Endothelium + basement membrane
    • Tunica media: Smooth muscle + elastic fibers (thickest in arteries)
    • Tunica adventitia: Connective tissue

Types of Arteries:

  • Elastic arteries (aorta, pulmonary): Dampen pressure fluctuations.
  • Muscular arteries (femoral, renal): Distribute blood to organs.
  • Arterioles: Regulate blood flow into capillaries (resistance vessels).

🕸️ B. Capillaries

  • Microscopic vessels (one cell thick)
  • Site of exchange between blood and tissues
Type Characteristics Location
Continuous Tight junctions; small gaps (4 nm) Muscles, lungs, CNS
Fenestrated Pores (60-80 nm) in endothelium Kidneys, intestines, endocrine glands
Sinusoidal Large gaps, incomplete basement membrane Liver, spleen, bone marrow

🟦 C. Veins

  • Carry blood toward the heart
  • Thinner walls, larger lumen than arteries
  • Contain valves (in limbs) to prevent backflow
  • Capacitance vessels (hold ~60% of blood volume)

📊 Blood Pressure & Cardiac Cycle

Blood pressure = Force exerted by blood against vessel walls

🔄 Cardiac Cycle and Pressure

Phase Ventricular Pressure Aortic Pressure AV Valves Semilunar Valves
Ventricular systole Rises (80-120 mmHg) 80-120 mmHg Closed Open
Ventricular diastole Falls (0-10 mmHg) Drops to ~80 mmHg Open Closed

📈 Blood Pressure Values

  • Systolic pressure: Peak pressure during ventricular contraction (~120 mmHg)
  • Diastolic pressure: Minimum pressure during ventricular relaxation (~80 mmHg)
  • Pulse pressure = Systolic - Diastolic (~40 mmHg)
  • Mean arterial pressure (MAP) = Diastolic + 1/3 Pulse pressure

⚡ Factors Affecting Blood Pressure

MAP = Cardiac Output × Total Peripheral Resistance

Factor Effect on BP
↑ Cardiac output ↑ BP
↑ Peripheral resistance ↑ BP
↑ Blood volume ↑ BP
↑ Blood viscosity ↑ BP
↑ Venous return ↑ BP

➕➖ Rh Factor (Rhesus System)

  • Rh-positive: Have D antigen (~85% of population)
  • Rh-negative: Lack D antigen (~15%)
⚠️ Clinical significance — Hemolytic Disease of Newborn (HDN):
Occurs when an Rh-negative mother carries an Rh-positive baby.
  • First pregnancy: Sensitization occurs during delivery.
  • Subsequent pregnancy: Maternal anti-D antibodies cross placenta and destroy fetal RBCs.
  • Prevention: RhoGAM (anti-D antibodies) given to mother.

🧪 Blood Composition & Functions

🩸 Components of Blood

1. Plasma (55%):

Water 92% Solvent
Proteins 7% Albumin, globulins, fibrinogen
Electrolytes <1% Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, Cl⁻, HCO₃⁻
Nutrients <1% Glucose, amino acids, lipids

Gases <1% O₂, CO₂

2. Formed Elements (45%):

Cell Type Number per µL Function Lifespan
Erythrocytes (RBCs) 4.5-5.5 million O₂ and CO₂ transport 120 days
Leukocytes (WBCs) 4,000-11,000 Immunity Hours to years
Thrombocytes (Platelets) 150,000-450,000 Hemostasis 7-10 days

🛡️ Types of White Blood Cells

Cell Type Percentage Function
Neutrophils 50-70% Phagocytosis of bacteria
Lymphocytes 20-40% B cells (antibodies), T cells (cell-mediated), NK cells
Monocytes 2-8% Macrophages (phagocytosis, antigen presentation)
Eosinophils 1-4% Parasitic infections, allergic reactions
Basophils 0.5-1% Release histamine (allergic responses)

🛡️ Functions of Blood

  • Respiratory: O₂ transport (hemoglobin), CO₂ transport (as bicarbonate).
  • Nutritive: Transport absorbed nutrients.
  • Excretory: Transport wastes to kidneys.
  • Regulatory: Hormone transport, pH regulation (bicarbonate buffer).
  • Protective: Clotting, immunity.
