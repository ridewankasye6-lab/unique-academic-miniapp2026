Complete Study Notes — Comprehensive Guide to Tenses

⏳ Tenses Overview

Tenses denote the time of action. They show when the work is done. They are:

  1. Present Tense
  2. Past Tense
  3. Future Tense

🌞 Present Tense

1. Simple Present Tense

It is used to denote scientific facts, universal truths and work done on daily basis.

⚙️ Structural Rules:
• Assertive: Sub + V1 + s/es + Object
• Negative: Sub + does not + V1 + s/es + Object
• Interrogative: Does + Sub + V1 + s/es + Object
• Interrogative Negative Assertive: Does + Sub + not + V1 + s/es + Object
💡 Examples:
• Assertive: She writes a letter.
• Negative: She does not write a letter.
• Interrogative: Does she write a letter?
• Interrogative Negative: Does she not write a letter?
🎯 4 Common Real-Life Usages:
  • 1. Daily Habits & Routines: ☕ "I drink coffee every morning at 7 AM."
  • 2. General Truths & Facts: 🌍 "The earth revolves around the sun."
  • 3. Fixed Timetables & Schedules: 🚆 "The university train leaves at 8:00 AM."
  • 4. Permanent States: 🏠 "My brother lives in Addis Ababa."

2. Present Continuous Tense

It is used to express an action taking place at the time of speaking.

⚙️ Structural Rules:
• Assertive: Sub + is/am/are + V1 + ing + Object
• Negative: Sub + is/am/are + not + V1 + ing + Object
• Interrogative: Is/am/are + Sub + V1 + ing + Object
• Interrogative Negative: Is/am/are + Sub + not + V1 + ing + Object
💡 Examples:
• Assertive: She is writing a letter.
• Negative: She is not writing a letter.
• Interrogative: Is she writing a letter?
• Interrogative Negative: Is she not writing a letter?
🎯 4 Common Real-Life Usages:
  • 1. Action Happening Right Now: 📱 "Shh! I am speaking to my professor on the phone."
  • 2. Temporary Situations: 🏢 "She is working at the university library this summer."
  • 3. Changing or Developing Situations: 📈 "Your English fluency is improving day by day."
  • 4. Fixed Future Arrangements: ✈️ "We are meeting the legal team tomorrow evening."

3. Present Perfect Tense

It is used to show an action that started in the past and has just finished.

⚙️ Structural Rules:
• Assertive: Sub + has/have + V3 + Object
• Negative: Sub + has/have + not + V3 + Object
• Interrogative: Has/have + Sub + V3 + Object
• Interrogative Negative: Has/have + Sub + not + V3 + Object
💡 Examples:
• Assertive: She has written a letter.
• Negative: She has not written a letter.
• Interrogative: Has she written a letter?
• Interrogative Negative: Has she not written a letter?
🎯 4 Common Real-Life Usages:
  • 1. Recently Completed Actions: 📩 "I have just submitted my law assignment."
  • 2. Life Experiences (Time Unspecified): ✈️ "She has traveled to three different countries."
  • 3. Past Action with Present Result: 🔑 "He has lost his keys, so he cannot enter the room."
  • 4. Action Continuing Up to Present: ⏳ "We have known each other for five years."

4. Present Perfect Continuous Tense

This tense shows the action which started in the past and is still continuing.

⚙️ Structural Rules:
• Assertive: Sub + has/have + been + V1 + ing + Object
• Negative: Sub + has/have + not been + V1 + ing + Object
• Interrogative: Has/have + Sub + been + V1 + ing + Object
• Interrogative Negative: Has/have + Sub + not + been + V1 + ing + Object
💡 Examples:
• Assertive: She has been writing a letter.
• Negative: She has not been writing a letter.
• Interrogative: Has she been writing a letter?
• Interrogative Negative: Has she not been writing a letter?
🎯 4 Common Real-Life Usages:
  • 1. Action Started in Past and Ongoing: 📚 "I have been studying since 6 AM today."
  • 2. Emphasizing Duration of Action: ⏳ "She has been coding this mini-app for 3 hours."
  • 3. Action Recently Stopped with Visible Result: 🌧️ "It has been raining, so the grass is wet."
  • 4. Temporary Repeated Actions: 🏃‍♂️ "He has been practicing shadowing techniques lately."

📜 Past Tense

Tense symbolizes the ever moving, non-stop wheel of time which is forever busy gathering moments of future and throwing them into the dustbin of past.

1. Simple Past Tense

Used to indicate an action completed in the past. It often occurs with an adverb of time, but sometimes without. Also used for past habits.

⚙️ Structural Rules:
• Assertive: Subject + V2 + Object + (.)
• Negative: Subject + didn’t + V1 + Object + (.)
• Interrogative: Did + Subject + V1 + Object + (?)
• Interrogative Negative: Did + Subject + not + V1 + Object + (?)
💡 Examples:
• I played football when I was a child.
• Assertive: She wrote a letter.
• Negative: She didn’t write a letter.
• Interrogative: Did she write a letter?
• Interrogative Negative: Did she not write a letter?
🎯 4 Common Real-Life Usages:
  • 1. Completed Action at Specific Past Time: 🎓 "I passed my exam last Friday."
  • 2. Series of Completed Past Actions: 🔑 "He entered the office, sat down, and opened his laptop."
  • 3. Past Habits or States: ⚽ "I played football every weekend when I was a child."
  • 4. Single Event in Past History: 📜 "The contract was signed in 2024."

2. Past Continuous Tense

Used to denote an action going on at some time in the past.

⚙️ Structural Rules:
• Assertive: Subject + was/were + V1 + ing + Object + (.)
• Negative: Subject + was/were + not + ing + Object + (.)
• Interrogative: Was/were + Subject + ing + Object + (?)
• Interrogative Negative: Was/were + Subject + not + ing + Object + (?)
💡 Examples:
• I was driving a car.
• Assertive: She was writing a letter.
• Negative: She was not writing a letter.
• Interrogative: Was she writing a letter?
• Interrogative Negative: Was she not writing a letter?
🎯 4 Common Real-Life Usages:
  • 1. Action in Progress at a Specific Time: 🚗 "At 8 PM yesterday, I was driving home."
  • 2. Interrupted Past Action: 📞 "I was studying when the phone rang."
  • 3. Two Simultaneous Past Actions: 📖 "While she was reading, her brother was writing."
  • 4. Setting the Scene in Storytelling: 🌧️ "The sun was setting and birds were singing as we walked."

3. Past Perfect Tense

Used to describe an action completed before a certain moment in the past, usually a long time ago. If two actions happened in the past, past perfect is used to show the action that took place earlier.

⚙️ Structural Rules:
• Assertive: Subject + had + V3 + Object + (.)
• Negative: Subject + had + not + Object + (.)
• Interrogative: Had + Subject + V3 + Object + (?)
• Interrogative Negative: Had + Subject + not + V3 + Object + (?)
💡 Examples:
• The patient had died before the doctor came.
• Assertive: She had written a letter.
• Negative: She had not written a letter.
• Interrogative: Had she written a letter?
• Interrogative Negative: Had she not written a letter?
🎯 4 Common Real-Life Usages:
  • 1. Action Completed Before Another Past Action: 👨‍⚕️ "The patient had died before the doctor arrived."
  • 2. Regrets / Unfulfilled Wishes: 💭 "I wish I had prepared better for the mid-term test."
  • 3. Reported Speech for Past/Present Perfect: 💬 "She said that she had already finished her assignment."
  • 4. Third Conditional Sentences: 🚗 "If I had taken the bus, I would not have been late."

4. Past Perfect Continuous Tense

Used to denote an action that began before a certain point in the past and continued up to some time in past.

⚙️ Structural Rules:
• Assertive: Subject + had been + V1 + ing + Object + (.)
• Negative: Subject + had + not been + V1 + ing + Object + (.)
• Interrogative: Had + Subject + been + V1 + ing + Object + (?)
• Interrogative Negative: Had + Subject + not + been + V1 + ing + Object + (?)
💡 Examples:
• I had been learning English in this school for 20 days.
• Assertive: She had been writing a letter.
• Negative: She had not been writing a letter.
• Interrogative: Had she been writing a letter?
• Interrogative Negative: Had she not been writing a letter?
🎯 4 Common Real-Life Usages:
  • 1. Ongoing Action Continuous Before Past Moment: 🏫 "I had been learning English for 20 days before exams started."
  • 2. Past Cause of a Past Result: 😴 "He was exhausted because he had been revising all night."
  • 3. Emphasizing Continuous Effort in Past: 💻 "They had been working on the website project for months."
  • 4. Duration Before Interruption in Past: 🌧️ "It had been raining for two hours before we stepped outside."

🚀 Future Tense

Time and tide wait for no man. So, a period of time following the moment of speaking or writing is called future tense.

📌 Usage Note for Shall/Will:
• In Future Tense, helping verb 'Shall' is used with 'I' and 'We'. Helping verb 'Will' is used with all others.
• When you are to make a commitment or warn someone or emphasize something, use of 'will/shall' is reversed. 'Will' is used with 'I' & 'We' and 'shall' is used with others.
• In general speaking there is hardly any difference between 'shall & will' and normally 'Will' is used with all.

1. Simple Future Tense

Tells us about an action which has not occurred yet and will occur after saying or in future.

⚙️ Structural Rules:
• Assertive: Subject + Will/Shall + Verb (1st form) + Object + (.)
• Negative: Subject + Will/Shall + Not + Verb (1st form) + Object + (.)
• Interrogative: Will/Shall + Subject + Verb (1st form) + Object + (?)
• Interrogative Negative: Will/Shall + Subject + Not + Verb (1st form) + Object + (?)
💡 Examples:
• Assertive: She will write a letter.
• Negative: She will not write a letter.
• Interrogative: Will she write a letter?
• Interrogative Negative: Will she not write a letter?
🎯 4 Common Real-Life Usages:
  • 1. Instant Decisions made at the moment: 🤝 "Wait, I will help you with those heavy books!"
  • 2. Predictions based on Opinion: 🌤️ "I think it will be sunny tomorrow afternoon."
  • 3. Promises & Commitments: 🤝 "I will send you the class PDF notes tonight."
  • 4. Offers & Requests: ☕ "Shall I bring you a cup of tea?"

2. Future Continuous Tense

Used to express an ongoing or continued action in future. We use it to talk about something that will be in progress at or around a time in the future.

⚙️ Structural Rules:
• Assertive: Subject + Will/Shall + Be + Verb (1st form) + Ing + Object + (.)
• Negative: Subject + Will/Shall + Not + Be + Verb (1st form) + Ing + Object + (.)
• Interrogative: Will/Shall + Subject + Be + Verb (1st form) + Ing + Object + (?)
• Interrogative Negative: Will/Shall + Subject + Not + Be + Verb (1st form) + Ing + Object + (?)
💡 Examples:
• He will be distributing sweets in temple tomorrow at 12 o'clock.
• They will be staying for a week.
• Assertive: She will be writing a letter.
• Negative: She will not be writing a letter.
• Interrogative: Will she be writing a letter?
• Interrogative Negative: Will she not be writing a letter?
🎯 4 Common Real-Life Usages:
  • 1. Action in Progress at Specific Future Time: 🏟️ "This time tomorrow, I will be watching the match in stadium."
  • 2. Planned or Expected Future Events: 🧳 "They will be staying with us for a whole week."
  • 3. Polite Inquiries about Plans: 🚙 "Will you be driving to campus tomorrow morning?"
  • 4. Parallel Future Actions: 📖 "She will be studying while he will be cooking dinner."

3. Future Perfect Tense

Used to express an action which will happen/occur in future and will be completed by a certain time in future.

⚙️ Structural Rules:
• Assertive: Subject + Will/Shall + Have + Verb (3rd form) + Object + (.)
• Negative: Subject + Will/Shall + Not + Have + Verb (3rd form) + Object + (.)
• Interrogative: Will/Shall + Subject + Have + Verb (3rd form) + Object + (?)
• Interrogative Negative: Will/Shall + Subject + Not + Have + Verb (3rd form) + Object + (?)
💡 Examples:
• They will have shifted the house by Sunday morning.
• Assertive: She will have written a letter.
• Negative: She will not have written a letter.
• Interrogative: Will she have written a letter?
• Interrogative Negative: Will she not have written a letter?
🎯 4 Common Real-Life Usages:
  • 1. Action Completed Before Specific Future Time: 📦 "They will have shifted house by Sunday morning."
  • 2. Expressing Assumptions about Recent Past: 🚌 "The bus will have left by now, so let's take a taxi."
  • 3. Milestones Achieved in Future: 🎓 "By next year, I will have graduated from university."
  • 4. Deadlines with 'By' or 'By the time': 📝 "She will have finished her thesis by midnight."

4. Future Perfect Continuous Tense

Used to talk about actions that will commence at a fix time in future and will continue for some time in future. Emphasizes the duration of an activity that will be in progress before another time or event in the future.

⚠️ Crucial Rule: If there is no time reference, then it is not a Future perfect continuous tense. Without continued time reference, such sentences are Future Continuous Tense.
⚙️ Structural Rules:
• Assertive: Subject + Will/Shall + Have been + Verb (1st form) + Ing + Object + (.)
• Negative: Subject + Will/Shall + Not + Have been + Verb (1st form) + Ing + Object + (.)
• Interrogative: Will/Shall + Subject + Have been + Verb (1st form) + Ing + Object + (?)
• Interrogative Negative: Will/Shall + Subject + Not + Have been + Verb (1st form) + Ing + Object + (?)
💡 Examples:
• This time tomorrow, I will be enjoying the cricket match in the stadium.
• Assertive: She will have been writing a letter.
• Negative: She will not have been writing a letter.
• Interrogative: Will she have been writing a letter?
• Interrogative Negative: Will she not have been writing a letter?
🎯 4 Common Real-Life Usages:
  • 1. Emphasizing Duration Up to a Future Point: ⏳ "By 5 PM, I will have been working on this code for 8 hours."
  • 2. Expressing Cause of Future Result: 😴 "He will be tired because he will have been driving all day."
  • 3. Ongoing Continuous Action Before Future Event: 🏁 "When she graduates, she will have been living here for 4 years."
  • 4. Long-term Work/Life Continuity: 🏢 "By December, my father will have been working in this firm for 20 years."

🎯 University Level Mixed Tenses Practice

Test your knowledge with these 10 advanced, mixed-tense examination questions based directly on our real-life usage concepts. Answers are provided directly below each item.

  1. Question 1 (Usage: Daily Routines vs. Actions Right Now): My roommate usually ____________ (drink) tea every morning at 7 AM, but today she ____________ (drink) coffee to stay awake for her exam.
    ✅ Answer: drinks | is drinking
    Explanation: Simple Present ("drinks") for a daily habit/routine paired with Present Continuous ("is drinking") for a temporary action right now.
  2. Question 2 (Usage: Action in Progress Interrupted in Past): While the student software developer ____________ (code) the mini-app features last night, the electricity suddenly ____________ (go) off.
    ✅ Answer: was coding | went
    Explanation: Past Continuous ("was coding") for an ongoing past action interrupted by a completed Simple Past action ("went").
  3. Question 3 (Usage: Scientific Facts & Universal Truths): The legal professor reminded the class that justice ____________ (require) fairness and water ____________ (boil) at 100°C under normal atmospheric pressure.
    ✅ Answer: requires | boils
    Explanation: Simple Present Tense is strictly used to state general facts and universal scientific truths.
  4. Question 4 (Usage: Actions Continuing Up to Present): We ____________ (know) each other since our first year of university, and we ____________ (study) law together for two years.
    ✅ Answer: have known | have been studying
    Explanation: Present Perfect ("have known" - stative verb) for duration up to present, and Present Perfect Continuous ("have been studying") emphasizing ongoing active duration.
  5. Question 5 (Usage: Action Completed Before Another Past Action): The defense team claimed that the defendant ____________ (leave) the city two hours before the crime ____________ (occur).
    ✅ Answer: had left | occurred
    Explanation: Past Perfect ("had left") indicates the earlier of two completed past actions before the Simple Past event ("occurred").
  6. Question 6 (Usage: Actions In Progress at Specific Future Time): Don't call me tomorrow at 12 o'clock because I ____________ (distribute) study notes to my classmates at the stadium.
    ✅ Answer: will be distributing
    Explanation: Future Continuous Tense used for an action that will be in progress at a specific time in the future.
  7. Question 7 (Usage: Completion Before Specific Future Deadline): By next Sunday morning, the moving team ____________ (shift) all the office furniture to the new branch.
    ✅ Answer: will have shifted
    Explanation: Future Perfect Tense expresses an action that will be fully completed before a specific future deadline ("By next Sunday morning").
  8. Question 8 (Usage: Duration continuous before a past point): Before the doctor finally arrived at the clinic, the patient ____________ (wait) in pain for over three hours.
    ✅ Answer: had been waiting
    Explanation: Past Perfect Continuous Tense highlights an ongoing activity that continued up to a specific past moment.
  9. Question 9 (Usage: Promises & Commitments): I strictly promise that I ____________ (send) you the English shadowing audio practice file first thing tomorrow morning.
    ✅ Answer: will send
    Explanation: Simple Future Tense using "will" expresses a direct commitment or promise made to someone.
  10. Question 10 (Usage: Duration up to a future point): By 5 PM this evening, my fellow university developers ____________ (design) web templates continuously for ten hours.
    ✅ Answer: will have been designing
    Explanation: Future Perfect Continuous Tense emphasizes the ongoing duration of an activity up to a specific future point (includes time reference "for ten hours").
