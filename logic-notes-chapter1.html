📚 CHAPTER ONE: INTRODUCING PHILOSOPHY

🔍 What Is Philosophy?

Philosophy is the study of general and fundamental problems concerning matters such as:

  • 🌌 Existence (What does it mean to "be"? Do we really exist?)
  • 🔍 Knowledge (How do we know what we know?)
  • 🔮 Truth (What is true? How do we prove something is true?)
  • 🎨 Beauty (What makes something beautiful?)
  • ⚔️ Law and Justice (What is fair or just?)
  • 🗣️ Mind and Language (How do we think and communicate?)

Logic is a branch of philosophy which is treated simultaneously as a field of study and as an instrument:

  • It studies arguments and the principles and methods of right reasoning.
  • As an instrument, it helps us to formulate our own rational arguments and critically evaluate the soundness of others' arguments.

✨ Meaning and Nature of Philosophy

🤔 Why is it hard to define?

It is difficult to define philosophy because philosophy has no specific subject matter, but it primarily deals with fundamental issues.

  • Etymology: The word philosophy comes from two Greek words: philo (love) and sophia (wisdom). Thus, the literal definition is "love of wisdom". However, this literal definition is not sufficient to fully understand philosophy.
  • Origin: Pythagoras was the first to use the word philosopher to refer to a person who clearly shows a marked curiosity in the things he experiences and analyzes.
  • Philosophy refers to the development of critical habits, the continuous search for truth, and the questioning of the apparent.
💡 How to Learn Philosophy:
The best way to learn and understand philosophy is to philosophize:
  • To be confronted with philosophical questions and use philosophical language.
  • To become acquainted with differing philosophical positions and maneuvers.
  • To read the philosophers themselves.
  • To grapple with the issues for oneself.

The wisdom that philosophers seek is not the wisdom of technical expertise or professional skills. Philosophy involves reason, rational criticism, and rigorous examination.

For Socrates, philosophy is a pursuit of wisdom through:

  • The development of critical habits.
  • The continuous search for truth.
  • The questioning of the apparent.

Definition: Philosophy is a rational and critical enterprise that tries to formulate and answer fundamental questions through an intensive application of reason that draws on analysis, comparison, and evaluation.

⚖️ Two Sides of Philosophy
  • 🛠️ Constructive Side: Attempts to formulate rational answers to fundamental questions concerning:
    • The nature of reality
    • The nature of value
    • The nature of knowledge and truth
  • 🔍 Critical Side: Deals with providing rational critique, analysis, clarification, and evaluation of answers given to basic metaphysical, epistemological, and axiological questions.
📍 Important Note: Philosophy is an activity. It is not something that can be easily mastered or learned passively in schools.

📌 Basic Features of Philosophy

1
Set of Views or Beliefs 🌐
Philosophy is a set of views or beliefs about life and the universe (informal sense of philosophy or "having" a philosophy).
2
Process of Reflecting 🔍
Philosophy is a process of reflecting on and criticizing our most deeply held conceptions and beliefs (formal sense of "doing" philosophy). Note: Having and doing philosophy cannot be treated entirely independently of each other.
3
Rational Worldview 🌍
Philosophy is a rational attempt to look at the world as a whole. It seeks to combine the conclusions of various sciences and human experience into a consistent worldview.
4
Logical Analysis 🗣️
Philosophy is the logical analysis of language and the clarification of the meaning of words and concepts.
5
Perennial Problems ❓
Philosophy is a group of perennial/constant problems that interest people and for which philosophers have always sought answers. It presses inquiry into deep existential questions (e.g., What is life and why am I here? Where does knowledge come from?).

Philosophy is better seen as asking the right questions rather than providing final correct answers.

Generally, philosophy encompasses the various theories or systems of thought developed by great thinkers such as Socrates, Plato, and Aristotle.

🧠 Core Fields of Philosophy

🔍 Digging Deep Into the Foundations of Philosophical Thinking

Philosophy has both primary and secondary branches. This course focuses on the primary core fields:

  1. 🌌 Metaphysics — The study of existence and reality.
  2. 🔮 Epistemology — The study of knowledge.
  3. 💎 Axiology — The study of value (ethics and aesthetics).
  4. ⚡ Logic — The study of reasoning and arguments.

🌌 Metaphysics

Derived from Greek "meta" (beyond/after) and "physika" (physics). Metaphysics studies the ultimate nature of reality or existence, addressing cause-and-effect relationships and reality vs. appearance.

Questions Metaphysics Asks:

  • What is reality? What is life or time?
  • What is mind, and what is its relation to the body?
  • Is there a cause-and-effect relationship between reality and appearance?
  • Are human actions free, or predetermined by supernatural forces?
📚 Four Subfields / Aspects of Metaphysics

🌌 i) Cosmological Aspect

Deals with the origin, nature, and development of the universe as an orderly system.

Questions: "How did the universe originate and develop? Does its existence have a purpose?"

🙏 ii) Theological Aspect

The part of religious theory dealing with conceptions of God and God's relationship to humans.

👤 iii) Anthropological Aspect

Deals with the study of human beings:

  • What is the relation between mind and body?
  • Is mind more fundamental than body, or vice versa?
  • Are people born good, evil, or morally neutral?
  • Do humans have free will, or are actions determined by environment, inheritance, or God?

🔬 iv) Ontological Aspect

Ontology is the study of the nature of existence itself (what it means for anything to exist):

  • Is reality orderly in itself, or merely ordered by the human mind?
  • Is reality fixed and stable, or is change its central feature?

🔮 Epistemology

Derived from Greek "episteme" (knowledge/understanding) and "logos" (study of). Referred to as the "theory of knowledge", it studies the nature, scope, sources, validity, and possibility of knowledge.

Core Epistemological Issues:

  • Can reality be known? Skepticism (Agnosticism) claims humans cannot acquire reliable knowledge and that any search for ultimate truth is in vain.
  • Is truth relative or absolute? Relative truth changes and varies, whereas absolute truth remains invariant.
🗝️ 5 Major Sources of Knowledge
  1. 👀 Empiricism (Empirical Knowledge): Knowledge obtained through the senses. Weakness: Senses can be incomplete or distorted by fatigue or limitations. Advantage: Experiences and experiments are open to replication and public examination.
  2. 🧠 Rationalism (Reason): Claims sensory experience alone cannot provide universal judgments, and humans can arrive at certainty through reason independently of senses.
  3. ⚡ Intuition: Direct apprehension of knowledge without conscious reasoning or immediate sense perception. Bypass limitations of human experience, though unsafe when used completely alone.
  4. 📜 Revelation: Knowledge communicated from a transcendent supernatural reality. Essential in religion; accepted by faith.
  5. 📚 Authority: Knowledge originating from experts, textbooks, or sanctified traditions. If built on wrong initial assumptions, authority-based knowledge becomes distorted.

💎 Axiology

Stems from Greek "axios" (value/worth) and "logos" (study of). Axiology is the philosophical study of value and worth, exploring three primary branches:

Branch Focus & Description
⚖️ Ethics Moral philosophy; studies principles, values, and rules used to evaluate conduct as good/bad or right/wrong.
🎨 Aesthetics Theory of beauty; examines art, enjoyment, sensory values, taste, and emotional perception.
🏛️ Social/Political Studies value judgments operating in civil society, government models, economic systems, and justice.

Categories of Ethics:

  • Normative Ethics: Establishes standards/principles to evaluate conduct (e.g., Teleological/Consequentialism, Deontology, Virtue Ethics).
  • Meta-Ethics: Investigates the meaning of ethical terms like "good", "bad", "right", or "wrong" (e.g., Intuitionism, Emotivism, Relativism).
  • Applied Ethics: Applies moral rules to specific practical issues (e.g., capital punishment, euthanasia, abortion).

⚡ Logic

Logic is the study or theory of principles of right reasoning. It formulates methods to evaluate the validity and soundness of arguments.

Key Questions Raised in Logic:

  • What is an argument, and what does it mean to argue?
  • What makes an argument valid or invalid?
  • What is a sound argument?
  • What is the relationship between premises and conclusions?
  • What is a fallacy, and what renders reasoning fallacious?

🌟 Importance of Learning Philosophy

🌹 "The unexamined life is not worth living." — Socrates

Philosophy gives us the tools to think deeply about life, society, and ourselves. It promotes self-actualization — self-fulfillment, creativity, and achieving one's potential.

1
Intellectual & Behavioral Independence 🧘‍♂️
Learn to think and act for yourself, integrating experiences, thoughts, and actions.
2
Reflective Self-Awareness 💎
Helps critically examine the essential intellectual grounds of our lives.
3
Flexibility, Tolerance & Open-Mindedness 🌍
Teaches tolerance and empathy towards views that clash with our own.
4
Creative & Critical Thinking 🧠
Refines powers of analysis, critical evaluation, reasoning, and argumentation.
5
Thought-Out Value Systems ⚖️
Provides an opportunity to formulate meaningful evaluation systems in morality, art, and politics.
