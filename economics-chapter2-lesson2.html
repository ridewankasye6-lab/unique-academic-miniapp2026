📊 Chapter 2 — Theory of Demand and Supply

📖 Lesson 2 — Supply

I. Related Goods

There are two types of related goods: substitute and complementary goods.

ሁለቱ አይነት ተያያዥ ምርቶች የሚገለጹበት አውድ substitute (ተካኪ) እና complementary (ተጓዳኝ) ሸቀጥ ነው:: የሁለቱ ልዩነት ደግሞ የተያያዥነታቸው ባህሪ ላይ ይወሰናል::

1. Substitute Goods (ተካኪ ሸቀጦች)

Substitute goods are goods which satisfy the same desire of the consumer. For example, tea and coffee, or Pepsi and Coca-Cola are substitute goods.

Substitute የምንለው እንግዛው የነበረን አንድ product ሊተካ የሚችል፣ በጥራትና ደረጃ ተመሳሳይ ወይም የተሻለ ሆኖ ነገር ግን ዋጋው በእንጻራዊነት የረከሰ ሌላ ሸቀጥ ሲኖር ነው::

ለምሳሌ፡ እኔ የምወደው Sun Chips ዋጋው በየጊዜው እየጨመረ ቢሄድ፣ ጥራቱ ተመሳሳይ የሆነና ዋጋው የተሻለ ሌላ ምርት (ለምሳሌ LoLi Chips) ቢመጣ፣ ወዲያው ከSun Chips ወደ LoLi Chips እቀይራለሁ::

If two goods are substitutes, then the price of one and the demand for the other are directly related.

ስለዚህ ሁለት ሸቀጦች እርስበርስ የሚተካኩ ከሆነ፣ የአንዳቸው ዋጋ ከሌላኛው ፍላጎት (demand) ጋር ቀጥታ የተገናኘ ነው:: የሁለተኛው demand በமுதல்ኛው ዋጋ ይወሰናል:: (ለምሳሌ፡ የCoca-Cola ዋጋ ሲጨምር የPepsi demand ይጨምራል::)

2. Complementary Goods (ተጓዳኝ ሸቀጦች)

Complementary goods, on the other hand, are those goods which are jointly consumed. For example, car and fuel, or tea and sugar are considered as complements.

Complementary የምንለው ደግሞ አንዱን ለመጠቀም ሌላኛው ግድ የሚያስፈልገን ተያያዥ ሸቀጥ ነው:: (ለምሳሌ፡ መኪና እና ነዳጅ)::

አንደኛውን የመግዛት አቅምና ፍላጎት ቢኖረንም (መኪና)፣ ሸቀጡን ለመጠቀም የሚያስፈልገው ሌላኛው ተጓዳኝ ምርት (ነዳጅ) ዋጋው በጣም ከተወደደ፣ ለመኪናው ያለው demand በእጅጉ ይቀንሳል::

If two goods are complements, then the price of one and the demand for the other are inversely related.

የአንዱ ተጓዳኝ ሸቀጥ ዋጋ እያደገ ሲሄድ፣ የሌላኛው ሸቀጥ ፍላጎት ይቀንሳል::

II. Determinants of Demand (Continued)

Number of Buyers in the Market

Since market demand is the horizontal sum of individual demands, an increase in the number of buyers will increase demand, while a decrease in the number of buyers will decrease demand.

የገዢዎች መበርከት ወይም ማነስ የሸቀጡን demand በእጅጉ ይወስነዋል:: ሰዎች ብዙ ሰው የሚሸምተውን ምርት መግዛት ይፈልጋሉ::

ስለዚህ ሸቀጡ በርካታ ተጠቃሚ እያገኘ ከመጣ demand እና ሽያጩ እየጨመረ ይመጣል:: ምክንያቱም በርካታ ተጠቃሚ ያለው ምርት ጥሩ ጥራትና ተቀባይነት አለው ተብሎ ስለሚታመን ነው::

III. Elasticity of Demand

In economics, the concept of elasticity is very crucial and is used to analyze the quantitative relationship between price and quantity purchased or sold.

Elasticity የኢኮኖሚክስ ወሳኝ ፅንሰ-ሀሳብ ሲሆን፣ በገበያ ላይ በሽያጭ/ፍላጎት (Demand) እና በዋጋ ተመን መካከል ያለውን አሃዛዊ ትስስር ለመተንተን ያገለግላል::

Elasticity is a measure of responsiveness of a dependent variable to changes in an independent variable.

Elasticity ማለት ለDemand ወሳኝ በሆኑ ምክንያቶች (እንደ ዋጋ ወይም የገቢ ለውጥ) ሸማቾች የሚሰጡት የግብረ-መልስ (response) መጠን ነው::

Elasticity of demand refers to the degree of responsiveness of quantity demanded of a good to a change in its price, change in income, or change in prices of related goods.

ስለዚህ Elasticity of Demand ማለት የሸቀጡ ዋጋ፣ የሸማቾች ገቢ ወይም የተያያዥ ሸቀጦች ዋጋ ሲለወጥ፣ ሸማቾች በግዢ መጠናቸው ላይ የሚያሳዩት የስሜትና የግብረ-መልስ መጠን ነው::

Commonly, there are three kinds of demand elasticity:

  1. Price elasticity of demand (የዋጋ Elasticity)
  2. Income elasticity of demand (የገቢ Elasticity)
  3. Cross elasticity of demand (የተያያዥ ሸቀጦች Elasticity)

1. Price Elasticity of Demand

Price elasticity of demand means the degree of responsiveness of demand to a change in price. It indicates how consumers react to changes in price. The greater the reaction, the greater will be the elasticity; the lesser the reaction, the smaller will be the elasticity.

ይህ ማለት ሸማቾች ለዋጋ ለውጥ እንዴት ምላሽ ይሰጣሉ የሚለውን ይገልጻል:: ለዋጋ ለውጥ የሚሰጡት ምላሽ ከፍተኛ ከሆነ Elasticityው ትልቅ ይሆናል፤ ምላሹ አነስተኛ ከሆነ ደግሞ Elasticityው ትንሽ ይሆናል::

Price elasticity of demand is calculated as the percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in price:

$$\epsilon = \frac{\% \text{ Change in Quantity Demanded}}{\% \text{ Change in Price}}$$

Demand for commodities like clothes and fruits changes significantly even with a small change in price (elastic). Whereas demand for basic necessities of life, like salt and food grains, may not change much even if prices change (inelastic).

መሰረታዊ የሆኑ የህይወት እቃዎች (እንደ ጤፍ፣ ጨው፣ ዳቦ) ዋጋ ቢጨምርም ሰዎች መግዛታቸውን ሙሉ በሙሉ ማቆም ስለማይችሉ Elasticityአቸው አነስተኛ ነው:: የቅንጦት ወይም አስፈላጊ ያልሆኑ እቃዎች ግን ዋጋቸው ሲጨምር ሰዎች በቀላሉ በመተዋቸው Elasticityአቸው ከፍተኛ ይሆናል::

Price elasticity of demand can be measured in two ways:

A) Point Price Elasticity of Demand

Calculated to find elasticity at a specific point on the demand curve:

$$\epsilon = \frac{\% \Delta Q}{\% \Delta P} = \frac{\frac{Q_2 - Q_1}{Q_1} \times 100}{\frac{P_2 - P_1}{P_1} \times 100}$$

Example: Suppose the price of a commodity falls from 5 Birr to 4 Birr ($P_1 = 5, P_2 = 4$), and quantity demanded increases from 100 units to 150 units ($Q_1 = 100, Q_2 = 150$).

$$\% \Delta Q = \frac{150 - 100}{100} \times 100 = 50\%$$ $$\% \Delta P = \frac{4 - 5}{5} \times 100 = -20\%$$ $$\epsilon = \frac{50\%}{-20\%} = -2.5$$

Interpretation: $|\epsilon| = 2.5$. This implies that at $P = 5\text{ Birr}$, a 1% decrease in price leads to a 2.5% increase in quantity demanded.

B) Arc Price Elasticity of Demand

The point elasticity method is applicable only for very small changes. In practice, when there are larger gaps in price and quantity, the Arc Elasticity method is used. It measures elasticity over a finite range (arc) using the average (midpoint) of old and new values.

$$\epsilon_{arc} = \frac{\frac{Q_2 - Q_1}{Q_1 + Q_2}}{\frac{P_2 - P_1}{P_1 + P_2}}$$

Example (using the same quiz numbers): $P_1 = 5, P_2 = 4, Q_1 = 100, Q_2 = 150$

$$\text{Numerator } (\Delta Q) = \frac{150 - 100}{100 + 150} = \frac{50}{250} = 0.2$$ $$\text{Denominator } (\Delta P) = \frac{4 - 5}{5 + 4} = \frac{-1}{9} \approx -0.111$$ $$\epsilon_{arc} = \frac{0.2}{-0.111} = -1.8$$

Values of Price Elasticity of Demand:

Elasticity Value Classification Description
$|\epsilon| > 1$ Elastic Demand Luxury products; consumers are highly responsive.
$0 < |\epsilon| < 1$ Inelastic Demand Necessities; consumers are less responsive.
$|\epsilon| = 1$ Unitary Elastic Demand Percentage change in quantity equals percentage change in price.
$|\epsilon| = 0$ Perfectly Inelastic Demand Quantity demanded does not change at all with price.
$|\epsilon| = \infty$ Perfectly Elastic Demand Infinitesimal price change causes infinite quantity change.

Determinants of Price Elasticity of Demand:

  • Availability of Substitutes: More substitutes $\rightarrow$ More elastic.
  • Time Period: Longer time horizon $\rightarrow$ More elastic (consumers adapt and substitutes emerge).
  • Proportion of Income Spent: Higher proportion of income spent $\rightarrow$ More elastic.
  • Nature of the Good: Luxuries $\rightarrow$ Elastic; Necessities $\rightarrow$ Inelastic.
  • Number of Uses: More uses for the commodity $\rightarrow$ More elastic.
  • Habits of Consumers: Habitual goods (e.g., cigarettes) $\rightarrow$ Inelastic (consumers buy regardless of reasonable price increases).

2. Income Elasticity of Demand

It measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in consumer income.

$$\epsilon_m = \frac{\% \Delta Q}{\% \Delta M} = \frac{Q_2 - Q_1}{M_2 - M_1} \times \frac{M_1}{Q_1}$$

Classification of Goods based on Income Elasticity ($\epsilon_m$):

Value Range Classification Description
$\epsilon_m > 1$ Luxury / Normal Good Demand grows faster than income.
$0 < \epsilon_m \le 1$ Necessity / Normal Good Demand increases with income, but at a slower rate.
$\epsilon_m < 0$ Inferior Good Demand decreases as income increases.

Example Calculation: Income ($M_1$) = 1000 Birr $\rightarrow$ Purchases ($Q_1$) = 4 kg wheat. Income ($M_2$) = 1200 Birr $\rightarrow$ Purchases ($Q_2$) = 5 kg wheat.

$$\% \Delta Q = \frac{5 - 4}{4} \times 100 = 25\%$$ $$\% \Delta M = \frac{1200 - 1000}{1000} \times 100 = 20\%$$ $$\epsilon_m = \frac{25\%}{20\%} = 1.25$$

Interpretation: $\epsilon_m = 1.25 > 1$, which indicates a 1% increase in income causes a 1.25% increase in demand. The commodity is a normal (luxury) good.

3. Cross Elasticity of Demand

Measures how much the quantity demanded of one good ($X$) responds to a change in the price of another good ($Y$).

$$\epsilon_{xy} = \frac{\% \Delta Q_x}{\% \Delta P_y} = \frac{Q_{x2} - Q_{x1}}{P_{y2} - P_{y1}} \times \frac{P_{y1}}{Q_{x1}}$$

Interpretation of Cross Elasticity Values:

Value Range Relationship
$\epsilon_{xy} > 0$ (Positive) Goods X and Y are Substitute goods.
$\epsilon_{xy} < 0$ (Negative) Goods X and Y are Complementary goods.
$\epsilon_{xy} = 0$ Goods X and Y are Unrelated goods.

Example Calculation: Price of $Y$ ($P_{y1}$) = 10 Birr $\rightarrow$ Demand for $X$ ($Q_{x1}$) = 1500 units. Price of $Y$ ($P_{y2}$) = 15 Birr $\rightarrow$ Demand for $X$ ($Q_{x2}$) = 1000 units.

$$\% \Delta Q_x = \frac{1000 - 1500}{1500} \times 100 = -33.33\%$$ $$\% \Delta P_y = \frac{15 - 10}{10} \times 100 = 50\%$$ $$\epsilon_{xy} = \frac{-33.33\%}{50\%} = -0.667$$

Interpretation: Since $\epsilon_{xy} = -0.667$ (negative), goods X and Y are complementary goods.

IV. Theory of Supply

Supply indicates the various quantities of a product that sellers (producers) are willing and able to provide at different prices in a given period of time, ceteris paribus (other things remaining unchanged).

አቅርቦት (Supply) ማለት አምራቾች/አቅራቢዎች በተለያዩ የዋጋ ተመኖች የተወሰነን ምርት ለገበያ ለማቅረብ ያላቸው ፍላጎትና አቅም ነው::

The Law of Supply States that, ceteris paribus, as the price of a product increases, the quantity supplied of that product increases; and as the price decreases, the quantity supplied decreases.

በLaw of Supply መሠረት የሸቀጡ ዋጋ ሲጨምር የቀረበው ምርት መጠን ይጨምራል፤ ዋጋ ሲቀንስ ደግሞ አቅርቦቱ ይቀንሳል (Direct relationship)::

Representations of Supply:

  • Supply Schedule: A tabular statement showing different quantities offered for sale at different prices.
  • Supply Curve: Graphical presentation of the supply schedule, sloping upward from left to right.
  • Supply Function: Mathematical statement representing the relationship between quantity supplied ($S$) and price ($P$): $S = f(P)$.
  • Market Supply: Derived by horizontally adding the quantities supplied of the product by all individual sellers at each price.

Determinants of Supply:

  • Input Prices: An increase in input costs (labor, raw materials) reduces supply (shifts supply curve leftward).
  • State of Technology: Technological advancement improves efficiency and increases supply (shifts supply curve rightward).
  • Prices of Related Goods: If a firm produces multiple goods, a price rise in another good may lead the firm to shift resources toward producing more of that good, decreasing the supply of the first.
  • Objectives of the Firm: Profit maximization vs. sales/market share expansion objectives influence supply volume.
  • Weather Conditions: Crucial for agricultural goods (favorable weather increases supply).
  • Other Factors: Infrastructure access, political stability, government fiscal policies (taxes and subsidies), and producer price expectations.

V. Elasticity of Supply

Elasticity of supply is the degree of responsiveness of quantity supplied to a change in price.

$$\epsilon_s = \frac{\% \text{ Change in Quantity Supplied}}{\% \text{ Change in Price}} = \frac{Q_2 - Q_1}{P_2 - P_1} \times \frac{P_1}{Q_1}$$
Elasticity of Supply ማለት አምራቾች ለዋጋ ለውጥ የሚያሳዩት የምርት መጠን ምላሽ ወይም ግብረ-መልስ ነው::
