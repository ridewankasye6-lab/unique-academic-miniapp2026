🌿 Nature and Characteristics of Human Rights

Human rights are fundamental rights and freedoms that belong to every person in the world, from birth until death. They apply regardless of where you are from, what you believe, or how you live.

🧠 Core Understanding

  • Human rights are inherent to all human beings
  • They are not granted by any state or authority
  • They cannot be taken away (except under specific legal conditions)
  • They are based on shared values like dignity, fairness, equality, and respect

🔒 Inalienable and Inviolable Rights

A. Inalienable Rights

Rights that cannot be taken away, surrendered, or transferred.

Characteristics:

  • Cannot be sold, given away, or renounced
  • Cannot be forfeited through action or inaction
  • Belong to the person inherently
💡 Examples: Right to life, Right to human dignity, Freedom from torture and slavery.

B. Inviolable Rights

Definition: Rights that are absolutely protected and cannot be violated under any circumstances.

Key Points:

  • These rights are absolute
  • Cannot be suspended even during states of emergency
  • Protected regardless of public interest or national security claims
📜 Under FDRE Constitution (Article 10): Human and democratic rights are inviolable and inalienable. The Constitution explicitly protects these rights from infringement.

C. Non-Derogable Rights

Even during states of emergency, certain rights cannot be suspended:

Article Right
Article 14 Right to life
Article 15 Protection of right to life
Article 18 Prohibition against inhuman treatment (torture, slavery)
Article 25 Right to equality
Article 39(5) Rights of Nations, Nationalities and Peoples

⚖️ Balance Scale: Human Rights Limitations vs Non-Derogable Absolute Protection Side

🌐 Universal Applicability

Human rights are universal—they apply to all people everywhere, regardless of nationality, race, gender, religion, or any other status.

🔑 Key Principles of Universality

Principle Explanation
Universal Birthright Every human being is born with these rights
Geographic Universality Rights apply in all countries and regions
Cultural Universality Rights transcend cultural differences
Temporal Universality Rights apply at all times, in all eras

⚠️ Limitations and Debates

  • Cultural Relativism: Some argue that rights should be interpreted within cultural contexts.
  • Implementation Gaps: Universal recognition does not mean universal enforcement.
  • Progressive Realization: Some rights (economic, social) may be realized progressively based on resources.

🇪🇹 Ethiopia's Commitment

  • FDRE Constitution incorporates universal human rights standards.
  • Ethiopia is party to major international human rights treaties.
  • Article 13 requires interpretation of rights in conformity with international instruments.

⚖️ Democratic Rights vs. Human Rights Distinction

A. Human Rights

Rights inherent to all human beings by virtue of their humanity.

Characteristics:

  • Pre-exist the state
  • Not dependent on citizenship
  • Cannot be granted or revoked by government
  • Include: right to life, freedom from torture, etc.

Source: Human dignity and nature

B. Democratic Rights

Rights that enable citizens to participate in the political process and governance of their country.

Characteristics:

  • Depend on existence of democratic system
  • Typically limited to citizens
  • Granted and structured by constitution and laws
  • Include: right to vote, right to run for office, etc.

Source: Constitutional provisions

🔗 Relationship Between the Two

  • Democratic rights are a subset of broader human rights.
  • Democratic rights enable exercise and protection of human rights.
  • Both are protected under the FDRE Constitution (Chapter Three).
📜 Article 10: Human and Democratic Rights
Article 10 of the FDRE Constitution states:
  1. "Human rights and freedoms, emanating from the nature of mankind, are inviolable and inalienable."
  2. "Democratic rights of citizens and peoples, guaranteed by this Constitution, shall be respected."
Implications:
  • Human rights are inherent and cannot be violated.
  • Democratic rights are constitutionally guaranteed.
  • Both must be respected by all organs of government.

📜 Specific Rights Under FDRE Constitution

⚖️ Due Process Rights

Due process refers to the legal requirement that the state must respect all legal rights owed to a person. It balances the power of law enforcement and protects individuals from the state.

A. Right to Life (Article 14-15)

Article 14: "Every person has the inviolable and inalienable right to life, the security of person and liberty."

Article 15: "Every person has the right to life. No person may be deprived of his life except as a punishment for a serious criminal offence determined by law."

Key Points:

  • Right to life is non-derogable.
  • Death penalty only for serious crimes as determined by law.
  • Cannot be imposed arbitrarily.

B. Right to Liberty and Security (Article 17)

Article 17(1): "No one shall be deprived of his or her liberty except on such grounds and in accordance with such procedure as established by law."

Key Points:

  • Freedom from arbitrary arrest and detention.
  • Detention only as provided by law.
  • Includes protection against unlawful imprisonment.

C. Habeas Corpus (Article 19)

Habeas corpus (Latin: "you shall have the body") is a legal principle requiring that a detained person be brought before a court to determine the legality of detention.

Right Description
Right to be informed Person arrested has right to be informed promptly and in detail of the nature and cause of the charge
Right to remain silent Cannot be compelled to make statements that may be used against them
Right to counsel Right to communicate with and be visited by legal counsel
Judicial review Must be brought before a court within 48 hours of arrest

D. Presumption of Innocence (Article 20(3))

Article 20(3): "During proceedings, accused persons have the right to be presumed innocent until proved guilty according to law."

Implications:

  • Burden of proof rests with prosecution.
  • Accused does not need to prove innocence.
  • Any doubt must benefit the accused.
  • Pre-trial detention is not punishment.

E. Right to be Informed of Charges (Article 20(1))

Article 20(1): "Accused persons have the right to be informed with sufficient particulars of the charge brought against them and to be given the charge in writing."

Purpose: Enables preparation of defense, prevents surprise at trial, and ensures fair proceedings.

👤 Rights of Accused Persons (Article 20)

"Accused persons have the right to be represented by legal counsel of their choice, and, if they do not have sufficient means to pay for it and miscarriage of justice would result, the state shall provide them with legal counsel at its own expense."

Key Elements:
  • Choice of counsel
  • State-funded counsel for indigent accused when justice requires
  • Access to counsel from the moment of arrest

🔒 Privacy, Honor, Reputation & Protection

A. Right to Privacy (Article 26)

Article 26(1): "Everyone has the right to privacy. This right shall include the right not to be subjected to searches of his home, person or property, or the seizure of any property under his personal possession."

Area Protection
Home No unreasonable searches
Person No unlawful bodily searches
Property No unlawful seizure
Correspondence Privacy of mail, telephone, and electronic communications
Personal Data Protection of personal information

Exceptions: Justified searches with warrant, public safety and crime prevention, as provided by law with compelling reasons.

B. Right to Honor and Reputation (Article 26)

Article 26(2): "Everyone has the right to the inviolability of his notes and correspondence including postal letters, and communications made by means of telephone, telecommunications and electronic devices."

Article 27 (freedom of religion) also implicitly protects honor and reputation in religious contexts.

Protections Include:

  • Protection against defamation
  • Protection against false accusations
  • Right to dignity

C. Search and Seizure Limitations

  • Searches require warrant except in exigent circumstances
  • Evidence obtained illegally may be inadmissible
  • Balancing public safety with privacy rights

⛔ Prohibition of Double Jeopardy (Article 23)

Double jeopardy is the prosecution of a person twice for the same criminal offense.

Article 23: "No person shall be liable to be tried or punished again for an offence for which he has already been finally convicted or acquitted in accordance with the criminal law and procedure."

Element Explanation
Same Offense Applies to same criminal act, not similar offenses
Final Judgment Applies after conviction or acquittal becomes final
Jeopardy Attaches At trial commencement (jury sworn or first witness heard)
Res Judicata Matter finally decided cannot be relitigated

Purpose: Prevents harassment of accused, ensures finality in criminal proceedings, protects against government overreach, and preserves judicial resources.

Exceptions (Not Double Jeopardy): Retrial after hung jury/mistrial, appeal by prosecution on questions of law (if acquittal stands), civil proceedings following criminal case, different sovereign prosecuting (limited applicability).

🎯 Affirmative Action

Affirmative action refers to measures designed to remedy past discrimination and achieve substantive equality by providing preferential treatment to historically disadvantaged groups.

📜 Constitutional Basis:
Article 35(3) on women's rights:
"The historical legacy of inequality and discrimination suffered by women in Ethiopia taken into account, women, in order to remedy this legacy, are entitled to affirmative measures. The purpose of such measures shall be to provide special attention to women so as to enable them compete and participate on the basis of equality with men in political, social and economic life as well as in public and private institutions."

Article 89(4/5) on economic objectives:
"Government shall take measures to ensure equal participation of women in all economic and social development and to provide particular support to them in all development endeavors."

🎯 Purpose of Affirmative Action

  • Remedy Past Discrimination: Address historical disadvantages
  • Level Playing Field: Enable genuine equal competition
  • Increase Representation: Ensure diverse participation in all spheres
  • Break Stereotypes: Challenge assumptions about group capabilities
  • Promote Inclusion: Integrate marginalized groups into mainstream

🛑 Limits of Affirmative Action

  1. Must be temporary (in principle)—until inequality is addressed
  2. Must be proportional—not more than necessary
  3. Cannot create reverse discrimination—should not unduly burden others
  4. Must be based on objective criteria—not arbitrary
  5. Must serve legitimate purpose—remedying inequality
💡 Examples in Practice:
  • Reserved seats for women in parliament
  • Preferential admission for historically disadvantaged groups
  • Special scholarships and training programs
  • Employment quotas in public service

📜 4. Rule of Law & Constitutionalism

The rule of law is the principle that all persons, institutions, and entities, including the government itself, are accountable to laws that are publicly promulgated, equally enforced, and independently adjudicated.

🏛️ Core Principles of Rule of Law

  • 👑 Supremacy of Law: Law is supreme over all persons and institutions.
  • ⚖️ Equality Before the Law: No one is above the law; all are equally subject to it.
  • 🎯 Accountability: Government officials are accountable under the law.
  • 📖 Legal Certainty: Laws are clear, publicized, and prospective.
  • 🔍 Fair Application: Laws are enforced consistently and without arbitrariness.
  • 🚪 Access to Justice: All persons have access to courts and legal remedies.
  • 🛡️ Due Process: Fair procedures in legal proceedings.
  • 🏛️ Independent Judiciary: Courts are free from external interference.

⚖️ Constitutional Government vs. Rule by Law

A. Constitutional Government

A government whose powers are limited by a constitution and that operates in accordance with constitutional principles.

  • Limited Government: Government powers are defined and restricted.
  • Constitutional Supremacy: Constitution is the supreme law of the land.
  • Separation of Powers: Power is distributed among branches.
  • Protection of Rights: Fundamental rights are guaranteed.
  • Judicial Review: Courts ensure constitutionality.
  • Accountability: Government is answerable to the people.

B. Rule by Law

A situation where law is used as an instrument of government control, but the government itself is not bound by law.

  • Law as Tool: Law used to control citizens, not government.
  • Government Above Law: Rulers are not subject to law.
  • Arbitrary Power: Decisions based on discretion, not legal constraints.
  • No Independent Judiciary: Courts serve government interests.
  • Rights Not Protected: Rights exist only at government pleasure.

🔍 5. Accountability & Transparency

A. Accountability

The obligation of government officials and institutions to answer for their actions, accept responsibility, and face consequences for failures.

Type Description Mechanism
Political Accountability Answerable to elected bodies and ultimately voters Elections, parliamentary questions
Legal Accountability Subject to judicial review and legal sanctions Courts, administrative tribunals
Financial Accountability Responsible for proper use of public funds Audits, budget oversight
Administrative Accountability Answerable within bureaucratic hierarchy Performance reviews, disciplinary procedures
Social Accountability Answerable to civil society and public Public hearings, citizen report cards

B. Transparency

Openness and accessibility of government information and decision-making processes to the public.

  • 📄 Access to Information: Citizens can obtain government documents.
  • 🚪 Open Meetings: Government deliberations open to the public.
  • 📊 Public Reporting: Regular publication of activities and results.
  • 👁️ Clear Procedures: Decision-making processes visible.
  • 🛡️ Whistleblower Protection: Those exposing wrongdoing are protected.

🏛️ Constitutional Basis in FDRE

📌 Article 12(1): "The conduct of affairs of government shall be transparent."
📌 Article 12(2): "Any public official or an elected representative is accountable for any failure in official duties."

🤝 6. Ethical Values & Social Harmony

A. Cultural Relativism vs. Ethnocentrism

1. Cultural Relativism

The principle that an individual's beliefs and activities should be understood in terms of their own culture, not judged against the criteria of another culture.

  • Non-Judgmental: Avoids imposing one culture's standards on another.
  • Contextual Understanding: Practices understood within cultural context.
  • Respect for Diversity: Acknowledges validity of different cultural practices.
  • Avoids Superiority Claims: No culture inherently superior to another.
✅ Advantages:
  • Promotes cross-cultural understanding
  • Reduces prejudice and discrimination
  • Facilitates harmonious coexistence
  • Respects cultural autonomy
⚠️ Limitations:
  • May excuse harmful practices
  • Can hinder universal human rights
  • May lead to moral relativism

2. Ethnocentrism

Judging other cultures by the standards of one's own culture, often with a belief in the superiority of one's own culture.

  • Cultural Superiority: Belief that own culture is best.
  • Negative Judgment: Other cultures viewed as inferior.
  • In-group Favoritism: Preference for own cultural group.
  • Out-group Hostility: Suspicion or hostility toward other groups.

❌ Effects: Prejudice and discrimination, intergroup conflict, misunderstanding of others, and cultural isolation.

🌈 Unity in Diversity

Unity in diversity is the concept of maintaining social cohesion and common purpose while respecting and celebrating cultural, ethnic, and religious differences.

  • 🆔 Shared Identity: Common national identity transcending differences.
  • 🤝 Mutual Respect: Respect for different cultures and traditions.
  • ⚖️ Equality: Equal treatment and dignity for all groups.
  • 🌐 Inclusion: All groups participate in national life.
  • 🕊️ Tolerance: Acceptance of differences.
  • 💬 Dialogue: Communication between groups.

🇪🇹 Ethiopia's Diversity

  • Over 80 ethnic groups
  • Multiple languages (with equal state recognition)
  • Diverse religious traditions and varied cultural practices

📜 Constitutional Framework

  • Article 8: Sovereignty resides in Nations, Nationalities and Peoples.
  • Article 39: Rights of Nations, Nationalities and Peoples to Self-determination, Language and culture, and Self-government.
  • Article 46: States delineated on basis of settlement patterns, language, identity.

🕊️ Tolerance and Compromise

1. Tolerance

Willingness to allow the existence of opinions or behaviors that one dislikes or disagrees with.

Levels of Tolerance:

  1. Mere Tolerance: Putting up with differences
  2. Respect: Recognizing value in differences
  3. Appreciation: Valuing diversity positively
  4. Celebration: Actively promoting diverse expressions

2. Compromise

An agreement reached by mutual concession, essential for democratic decision-making.

  • Both sides give something & gain something
  • Neither gets everything
  • Maintains long-term relationships
  • Role in Governance: Legislative negotiations, policy formation, conflict resolution, coalition building.

⚖️ 7. Justice Concepts

📊 Distributive Justice

Concerns the fair allocation of resources, benefits, and burdens among members of society.

  • ❓ Key Questions: Who gets what? How are resources distributed? What is a fair share? How should burdens be allocated?
  • 💼 Application Areas: Taxation (who pays how much), public services (who receives), social welfare (who benefits), educational opportunities, and healthcare resources.

🛠️ Corrective Justice

Concerns the rectification of wrongs or injuries, restoring the balance upset by wrongful conduct.

  • 🚨 Wrongdoing: Some act that harms another.
  • 👤 Responsibility: Wrongdoer should make amends.
  • 🔄 Rectification: Restoring victim to prior position.
  • ⚖️ Proportionality: Remedy proportional to wrong.

📋 Procedural Justice

Concerns the fairness of the processes used to make decisions and resolve disputes, regardless of the outcome.

  • 🏛️ Neutrality: Decision-makers impartial.
  • 🗣️ Voice: Affected parties can participate.
  • 🤝 Respect: Parties treated with dignity.
  • 🔒 Trustworthiness & Transparency: Open and understandable processes seen as fair.
  • 🔄 Consistency: Similar cases treated similarly.

🏛️ 8. Legal Concepts & Institutions

🛡️ The Ombudsman

An independent official appointed to investigate complaints against public authorities and ensure fair administration.

Etymology: Swedish word meaning "representative" or "agent"

  • 🏛️ Establishment in Ethiopia: Article 55(15) of the FDRE Constitution grants the House of Peoples' Representatives the power to establish the Office of the Ombudsman.
  • 📜 Establishing Law: Institution of the Ombudsman Proclamation No. 211/2000.

⚙️ Functions & Powers of the Ombudsman

  • Investigation: Investigates complaints against administrative bodies.
  • Inquiry: Inquires into maladministration on own initiative.
  • Recommendation: Recommends remedial action, disciplinary action, or policy changes.
  • Reporting: Reports findings and recommendations to parliament.
  • Mediation & Monitoring: Facilitates resolution of disputes and monitors implementation.

A. Roles of the Judiciary

  • Adjudication: Resolve disputes between parties.
  • Interpretation: Interpret and apply laws.
  • Review: Review administrative actions.
  • Protection of Rights: Safeguard individual and group rights.

💼 9. Basic Legal Concepts

💰 Tax Evasion vs. Tax Avoidance

A. Tax Evasion

Definition: The illegal non-payment or underpayment of taxes through deliberate concealment, misrepresentation, or fraud.

  • 🚫 Illegality & Intent: Violates tax laws; deliberate attempt to evade tax.
  • 🕵️ Deception: Hiding income, inflating deductions, keeping two sets of books, offshore accounts.
  • ⚖️ Nature: Criminal offense subject to fines, penalties, and imprisonment.

B. Tax Avoidance

Definition: The legal arrangement of financial affairs to minimize tax liability within the framework of the law.

  • ✅ Legality & Intent: Within legal boundaries; aims to minimize tax liability legally.
  • 📑 Transparency: Uses legal provisions, tax-advantaged accounts, deductions, and treaties.
  • 💼 Nature: Civil matter; may be challenged but not criminal.

Legality Illegal Legal

Consequences Fines, imprisonment May be challenged, adjusted
Ethics Unethical Ethically debatable

🗡️ Homicide Definitions

Homicide is the killing of one human being by another. Not all homicide is criminal; some is justified or excused.

Category Description Examples
Criminal Homicide Unlawful killing with culpable mental state Murder, manslaughter
Justified Homicide Lawful killing in specific circumstances Self-defense, execution of legal sentence
Excusable Homicide Killing without fault Accident, insanity
Non-Criminal Homicide Not subject to criminal liability War, lawful police action

⚖️ Degrees of Criminal Homicide

A. Murder: Unlawful killing with malice aforethought (intent to kill or cause grievous bodily harm).

  • 🥇 First-Degree Murder: Premeditated, deliberate intent (e.g., Planned killing).
  • 🥈 Second-Degree Murder: Intent to kill but without premeditation (e.g., Killing in sudden quarrel).
  • 🚨 Felony Murder: Death caused during commission of felony (e.g., Death during robbery).

B. Manslaughter: Unlawful killing without malice aforethought.

  • 🔥 Voluntary Manslaughter: Intentional killing in heat of passion with provocation (e.g., Killing spouse upon discovering adultery).
  • 🚗 Involuntary Manslaughter: Unintentional killing through recklessness or negligence (e.g., Death from drunk driving).

🏛️ 10. Public vs. Closed Court Sessions

📢 A. Public Trial Principle

The right to a public trial means court proceedings are open to the public and media.

  • 🔍 Transparency: Public scrutiny ensures fairness.
  • 👤 Accountability: Judges and officials visible to public.
  • 🤝 Public Confidence: Builds trust in the judicial system.
  • 🚫 Deterrence & Education: Public learns about legal process and consequences.
📌 Article 20(1) FDRE Constitution: "Accused persons have the right to a public trial by an ordinary court of law within a reasonable time after having been charged."

🔒 B. Closed Court Sessions

Proceedings held without public access, limited to parties, lawyers, and essential court personnel.

Grounds for Closed Sessions (Article 20(1) Exceptions):

  • ⚖️ Public Morality: Cases involving sensitive moral issues.
  • 🛡️ National Security: Matters affecting state security.
  • 🕊️ Peace and Order: Situations where public attendance might disrupt proceedings.
  • 🔐 Privacy of Parties: Protecting legitimate privacy interests.
  • 👶 Additional Grounds: Juvenile proceedings, sexual offenses, trade secrets/medical records, and witness protection.
