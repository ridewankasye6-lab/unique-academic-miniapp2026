🔍 Philosophy of Language

Let's begin with a big question: What is language, really?

  • According to Semiotics (the study of sign processes in communication), language is the manipulation and use of symbols in order to draw attention to signified content.
  • Language isn't just about speaking — it's about conveying ideas using signs, symbols, or sounds to represent real things or concepts.

Philosophy of language is the reasoned inquiry into the nature, origins, and usage of language.

Philosophy of language has been concerned with four central problems: the nature of meaning, language use, language cognition, and the relationship between language, logic and reality.

💭 Philosophers of language ask questions like:

  • 🤔 What is meaning?
  • 🌍 How does language refer to the real world?
  • 🧪 Is language learned or is it innate?
  • 🧩 How does the meaning of a sentence emerge out of its parts?

🗣️ Functions of Language in Everyday Life

Ordinary language serves various functions in our day-to-day life. These functions are almost unlimited. Thus, among other things, individuals use language:

  • ✅ To tell stories,
  • ✅ To ask questions,
  • ✅ To guess at answers / form hypotheses,
  • ✅ To launch verbal assaults,
  • ✅ To tell jokes, give directions, sing songs, issue commands, greet someone, and so on.

3 Main Linguistic Functions

In general, language has three main linguistic functions:

🟡 A. Expressive (Emotive) Function

It is a function of language which is important for individuals to express their feelings or emotions (both positive and negative feelings).

💭 It's not focused on facts, but on personal expression.

💬 Examples:
  • She is smart. 😊
  • I like my English teacher. ❤️
  • I hate him. 😠
  • I dislike Abebe. 🤬

These are not claims about objective reality, but personal emotional reactions.

🔵 B. Directive Function

It gives direction to the speaker or writer in order to pass orders, commands or instructions to others.

📢 Examples:
  • What is your name?
  • Leave me alone!
  • Do not close that door!
  • Give me your pen!

🟢 C. Cognitive (Informative) Function

It is used to convey information about the world's objective realities.

📌 Of these functions of language, the cognitive function of language is relevant and important for logic because logic evaluates arguments, and arguments are built on informative claims (statements that can be True or False).

📖 Examples:
  • Ethiopia has its own prestigious airlines. (True)
  • The capital city of the regional state of Afar is Hawassa. (False)
  • Lake Tana is found in Amhara region. (True)

🔤 Meaning of Terms

The reason why we study about definitions is:

  • Words have meanings.
  • Meanings are conveyed through definitions.
  • Sometimes the meaning of certain words in an argument is vague or ambiguous.
  • Logic evaluates arguments, an argument consists of a group of statements, and statements are made up of words.
📌 Definition of a Term: A term is any word or arrangement of words that may serve as the subject of a statement.

Terms consist of proper names, common names, and descriptive phrases:

  • Ayele
  • house
  • The first president of Ethiopia
  • John
  • Person
  • The king of England
  • South Ethiopia
  • Animal
  • Those who study hard
    • Proper Names Common Names Descriptive Phrases

    Words that are NOT terms include verbs, non-substantive adjectives, adverbs, prepositions, conjunctions, and all non-syntactic arrangements of words.

    ⚠️ Non-Terms Examples: dictatorial, run quickly, above and beyond, moreover, craves, cabbages, into again, the forest.

    🔍 Intension and Extension Meaning of Terms

    Every term has two types of meaning:

    1. Intensional Meaning (Connotation)

    📌 Refers to the qualities or attributes that the term connotes or implies.

    💡 Example: "Inventor" means a person who is clever, intuitive, creative, and imaginative.
    (Meaning is based on attributes, qualities, or essential characteristics).

    2. Extensional Meaning (Denotation)

    📌 Consists of the members of the class that the term denotes or refers to.

    💡 Example: "Inventor" means such as Thomas Edison, Alexander Graham Bell, and Samuel F.B. Morse.
    (Meaning is based on listing class members).
    🔑 Key Relationship: The intensional meaning of a term serves as the criteria for deciding what the extension consists of. That is why intensional meaning determines extensional meaning.

    🌐 Extensional Consistency & Empty Extension

    • The denotation (extensional meaning) of "human being" refers to all human beings in the universe, on which everybody agrees.
    • Empty Extension (Null Class): Occurs when a term denotes a class that has no living or current objective members in reality.
    • Things without current objective reference: myths, spiritual realities, extinct creatures, historical figures of the past.
    🦕 Empty Extension Examples:
    • "The current king of Ethiopia" (Empty class – no current king exists)
    • Dinosaur, Dragon, Satan, fictional characters.

    Satan is defined intensionally as "an evil spirit that causes people to suffer." Dinosaur is defined as "an extinct reptile of the Mesozoic era."

    📈 Order of Intension and Extension

    Terms can be placed in order based on increasing or decreasing attributes (intension) and class sizes (extension):

    • Increasing Intension: Attributes increase (class size gets smaller / more specific).
      📍 Example: Africa, East Africa, Ethiopia, Addis Ababa.
    • Decreasing Intension: Attributes decrease (reverse of increasing intension).
      📍 Example: Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, East Africa, Africa.
    • Increasing Extension: Class size gets larger / broader.
      📍 Example: Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, East Africa, Africa.
    • Decreasing Extension: Class size gets smaller.
      📍 Example: Africa, East Africa, Ethiopia, Addis Ababa.

    📘 Definitions and Their Purposes

    Many logicians define definition as a group of words that assigns a meaning to some word or group of words.

    🧩 Two Parts of Every Definition:
    • Definiendum: The word or group of words that is supposed to be defined.
    • Definiens: The word or group of words that does the defining or gives meaning to the definiendum.
    💡 Example: "Diffident" means lacking confidence in oneself.
    • Definiendum: "Diffident"
    • Definiens: "lacking confidence in oneself"

    🎯 Purposes of Definitions in Logic

    • To avoid confusion or misleading use of words and phrases;
    • To avoid obscurity, unintelligibility, subjectivity (bias), and complexity of words;
    • To introduce new words and to persuade others;
    • To avoid useless controversies, disputes, disagreements, and conflicts over the meaning of terms;
    • To prevent incorrect reasoning; and to develop the ability to reason logically.

    📗 Types of Definitions and Their Purpose

    In logic, there are five distinct types of definitions:

    1️⃣ Stipulative Definition

    📌 Assigns a meaning to a word for the first time. This involves either creating a new word or giving a new meaning to an old word.

    Key Features:

    • Meaning is created, not discovered.
    • Words are assigned arbitrarily (no "right" or "wrong" meaning).
    • Has no truth value (neither true nor false).
    • Used for new phenomena, scientific developments, innovations, or setting up secret codes.
    💡 Examples:
    • Crossing a male tiger and female lion produced an offspring named "Tigon".
    • Military Secret Codes: "Operation Barbarossa", "Operation Desert Storm", "Operation Sunset".

    2️⃣ Lexical Definition

    📌 Used to report the meaning that a word already has in a language. (Dictionary definitions).

    Purpose: To eliminate ambiguity.

    • Ambiguity: A word is ambiguous if it has more than one meaning in a context (e.g., "Light" = radiant energy OR light in weight; "Bank" = financial institution OR river edge).
    • Vagueness: A word is vague if its meaning is imprecise or unclear, making it impossible to tell where it applies (e.g., "rich", "poor", "love", "normal").

    3️⃣ Précising Definition

    📌 Provides a more precise, specific, exact, and restricting meaning to a term in order to reduce vagueness.

    Key Note: Unlike stipulative definitions, précisng definitions are not arbitrary; they must be appropriate and legitimate to the specific context (e.g., law, science, medicine).

    💡 Example: Defining "Poor" as "a person having a monthly income of less than Birr 150."

    4️⃣ Theoretical Definition

    📌 Assigns a meaning to a word by suggesting a theory that gives a certain characterization to the entities that the term denotes.

    Common in science and philosophy (e.g., "heat", "substance", "good", "justice", "mind").

    💡 Examples:
    • "Good" means the greatest happiness of the greatest number (Utilitarianism).
    • "Justice" means to give each individual what he or she deserves.

    5️⃣ Persuasive Definition

    📌 Intended to persuade or convince listeners/readers, influencing their attitude towards a certain point of view.

    Uses emotionally charged or value-laden words to arouse emotion.

    💡 Opposing Examples:
    • "Abortion" means the ruthless murdering of innocent human beings.
    • "Abortion" means a safe and established surgical procedure whereby a woman is relieved of an unwanted burden.

    🛠️ Techniques of Definition

    A. Extensional (Denotative) Definition Techniques

    Defines terms by listing or indicating the members of the class denoted by the definiendum.

    1. Demonstrative (Ostensive) Definition

    📌 Assigns meaning by pointing physically to the object. Uses gestures.

    👉 Example: Pointing to objects one by one and saying, "'Chair' means this and this and this."

    2. Enumerative Definition

    📌 Assigns meaning by naming individual members of the class (can be partial or complete).

    🏃 Example: "Athlete" means a person such as Haile G/silassie, Kenenisa Bekele, or Derartu Tulu.

    3. Definition by Subclass

    📌 Assigns meaning by naming subclasses of the class denoted by the term.

    🌲 Example: "Tree" means an oak, pine, elm, spruce, or maple.

    B. Intensional (Connotative) Definition Techniques

    Defines terms by describing essential attributes or characteristics.

    1. Synonymous Definition

    📌 The definiens is a single word that connotes the same attributes as the definiendum.

    💡 Examples: "Physician" means doctor | "Intentional" means willful | "Observe" means see.

    2. Etymological Definition

    📌 Assigns meaning by revealing the word's root or ancestry in its own or foreign languages (Greek, Latin, Old English, French).

    🏛️ Examples:
    • "Democracy" comes from Greek demos (people) + kratos (power).
    • "Captain" comes from Latin caput (head).
    • "License" comes from Latin licere (to be permitted).

    3. Operational Definition

    📌 Assigns meaning by specifying experimental procedures or operations performed. Frequently uses "if and only if".

    🧪 Example: A solution is an "Acid" if and only if litmus paper turns red when dipped into it.

    4. Definition by Genus and Difference

    📌 Assigns meaning by identifying a Genus (larger class) and a Specific Difference (attribute distinguishing species within that genus).

    Species (Definiendum) = Specific Difference + Genus
    Ice frozen water
    Husband married man
    Mother female parent
    Tiger large, striped, ferocious feline

    📏 Criteria / Rules for Lexical Definitions

    To avoid errors in logic, a lexical definition should follow these 8 fundamental rules:

    1. Rule 1: Conform to Grammatical Standards
      ❌ Incorrect: Vacation is when you don't have to go to work.
      ✅ Correct: "Vacation" means a period during which activity is suspended from work or school.
    2. Rule 2: Convey Essential Meaning
      ❌ Incorrect: "Human being" means a featherless biped.
      ✅ Correct: "Human being" means the rational animal that has the capacity to reason and speak.
    3. Rule 3: Neither Too Broad nor Too Narrow
      ❌ Too Broad: "Bird" means any warm-blooded animal having wings (includes bats).
      ❌ Too Narrow: "Bird" means warm-blooded, feathered animal that can fly (excludes ostriches).
    4. Rule 4: Avoid Circularity
      ❌ Incorrect: "Scientist" means anyone who engages in science.
    5. Rule 5: Be Affirmative, Not Negative (when possible)
      ❌ Negative: "Concord" means the absence of discord.
      ✅ Affirmative: "Concord" means harmony.
      (Note: Intrinsically negative words like "Bald", "Darkness", or "Death" are exceptions).
    6. Rule 6: Avoid Figurative, Obscure, Vague, or Ambiguous Language
      ❌ Figurative: "Architecture" means frozen music.
      ❌ Obscure/Needlessly Technical: "Bunny" means a mammalian of the family Leporidae...
    7. Rule 7: Avoid Affective / Emotional Terminology
      Avoid sarcastic, biased, or emotional language meant to influence attitudes.
    8. Rule 8: Indicate the Context
      Explicitly state the context when a word has multiple distinct contextual meanings.
      💡 Example: "Strike" means (in baseball) a pitch at which a batter swings and misses; "Strike" means (in fishing) a pull on a line made by a fish taking bait.
