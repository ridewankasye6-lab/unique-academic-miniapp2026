Constitutional Law & FDRE Government Structure

📋 Chapter Outline & Coverage

This chapter covers the essential constitutional framework of the Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia (FDRE) for Law COC examination preparation:

📜 Fundamental Principles of the FDRE Constitution

The Constitution of the Federal Democratic Republic of Ethiopia was adopted on December 8, 1994 and entered into force on August 21, 1995. It establishes Ethiopia as a federal state with a democratic system of government.

📖 Preamble Summary:

The Nations, Nationalities and Peoples of Ethiopia, committed to building a political community founded on the rule of law and capable of ensuring lasting peace and democratic order, adopted this Constitution as an instrument binding them in mutual commitment.

⚖️ Supremacy of the Constitution (Article 9)

Article 9(1) states: "The Constitution is the supreme law of the land. Any law, customary practice or a decision of an organ of state or a public official which contravenes this Constitution shall be of no effect."

🔑 Key Implications & Aspects

Aspect Explanation
Hierarchy of Laws All laws, regulations, and directives must conform to constitutional provisions
Nullity of Contradictory Laws Any law conflicting with the Constitution is automatically void and unenforceable
Binding Effect All citizens, organs of state, political organizations, and associations have the duty to observe the Constitution
Prohibition of Unconstitutional Assumption of Power It is prohibited to assume state power in any manner other than that provided under the Constitution
International Law All international agreements ratified by Ethiopia are an integral part of the law of the land

🔍 Practical Application

  • ✔️ Legislative Review: When a law conflicts with the Constitution, it must be declared null and void by the appropriate constitutional body.
  • ✔️ Judicial Decisions: Courts must apply constitutional provisions as the primary legal authority.
  • ✔️ Administrative Actions: Government decisions and actions must comply with constitutional requirements.

🏛️ Separation of Powers

Constitutional Framework

The FDRE Constitution establishes three distinct branches of government with separate functions:

Branch Primary Function Constitutional Basis
Legislative Law-making Articles 53-62
Executive Law implementation and administration Articles 72-79
Judiciary Law interpretation and adjudication Articles 78-81

🛡️ Checks and Balances

1. Legislative over Executive:

  • ✔️ Approval of government programs and budget
  • ✔️ Parliamentary questions and interpellations
  • ✔️ Vote of confidence/no-confidence

2. Executive over Legislative:

  • ✔️ Proposal of laws
  • ✔️ Implementation of laws
  • ✔️ Declaration of state of emergency (with legislative approval)

3. Judicial Independence:

  • ✔️ Courts free from interference by other branches
  • ✔️ Judges decide solely based on law and facts

🗺️ Federalism Structure

Ethiopia is a federal state consisting of nine regional states and two chartered cities administered by the Federal Government.

Characteristics of Ethiopian Federalism

  1. Dual Government Structure: Federal and state governments with constitutionally defined powers.
  2. Ethnic Federalism: Regional states largely delineated along ethnolinguistic lines.
  3. State Constitutions: Each state has its own constitution.
  4. Bicameral Legislature: Two houses at federal level representing the people and the nations/nationalities.
  5. Residual Powers: Powers not expressly given to federal government or concurrent list are reserved to states.

👑 Sovereignty of Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples (Article 8)

Article 8(1) states: "All sovereign power resides in the Nations, Nationalities and Peoples of Ethiopia."

Article 8 provides three ways sovereignty is expressed:

  • ✔️ The Constitution itself is an expression of their sovereignty
  • ✔️ Through representatives elected in accordance with the Constitution
  • ✔️ Through direct, democratic participation

Rights of Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples (Article 39)

Right Description
Self-Determination Every Nation, Nationality and People has the right to self-determination, including the right to secession
Language and Culture Right to speak, write, and develop their own language; express, develop, and promote their culture
Equality Right to full measures of self-government within their territory
Representation At least 20 seats in House of Peoples' Representatives for minority nationalities

🕊️ Secularism (Separation of State and Religion) (Article 11)

Constitutional Provisions

Article 11 establishes three fundamental principles:

  • ✔️ "State and religion are separate."
  • ✔️ "There shall be no state religion."
  • ✔️ "The state shall not interfere in religious matters and religion shall not interfere in state affairs."

Implications of Secularism

Aspect Meaning
Religious Neutrality State does not favor any religion over another
Non-Interference State does not involve itself in religious doctrines, leadership, or internal affairs
Religious Freedom Individuals free to practice religion without state interference
Equal Treatment All religions treated equally before the law
No Official Religion Ethiopia has no established or state religion
💡 Distinction from Related Concepts:
  • ✔️ Not Anti-Religious: Secularism does not mean opposition to religion.
  • ✔️ Not State Atheism: State does not promote atheism.
  • ✔️ Protects Both: Protects state from religious interference and religion from state control.

🔎 Conduct and Accountability of Government (Article 12)

Article 12(1) states: "The conduct of affairs of government shall be transparent."

Provision Explanation
Official Accountability Any public official or elected representative is accountable for any failure in official duties
Recall of Representatives In case of loss of confidence, the people may recall an elected representative
Public Access Citizens have right to access government activities, except where limited by law

🏛️ Section B: Organs of Government & Their Powers

Overview of Federal Government Structure.

🏛️ House of Peoples' Representatives (HPR)

A. Composition and Election (Article 54)

Membership:

  • ✔️ Not to exceed 550 members
  • ✔️ Members elected by the people for a term of five years
  • ✔️ Elected on the basis of universal suffrage by direct, free and fair elections held by secret ballot

Electoral System:

  • ✔️ Members elected from candidates in each electoral district by a plurality of votes cast (first-past-the-post)
  • ✔️ Single-member constituencies

Special Representation:

  • ✔️ Minority Nationalities and Peoples have at least 20 seats
  • ✔️ Provisions made by law for special representation for minority groups

Nature of Representation:

Members are representatives of the Ethiopian People as a whole. Governed by:

  1. The Constitution
  2. The will of the people
  3. Their Conscience

B. Privileges and Immunities

Immunity Description
Parliamentary Immunity No member may be prosecuted for any vote cast or opinion expressed in the House
Arrest Immunity No member may be arrested or prosecuted without permission of the House, except flagrante delicto
Recall Member may lose mandate upon loss of confidence by the electorate

C. Powers and Functions (Article 55)

Legislative Powers:

  • • Power of legislation in all matters assigned to Federal jurisdiction
  • • Enact specific laws on matters including:
    • ✔️ Utilization of land and natural resources
    • ✔️ Inter-state commerce and foreign trade
    • ✔️ Air, rail, and water transportation
    • ✔️ Postal and telecommunications services
    • ✔️ Enforcement of political rights
    • ✔️ Nationality, immigration, passport, and exit/entry permits
    • ✔️ Flag and other national symbols
    • ✔️ Standards and measurements
    • ✔️ Patents, copyrights, and trademarks

Financial Powers:

  • • Approve general policies and implementation strategies
  • • Approve federal budget
  • • Levy taxes and duties
  • • Ratify international agreements

Oversight Powers:

  • • Establish the Office of the Ombudsman and Human Rights Commission
  • • Question Prime Minister and other officials
  • • Conduct investigations
  • • Approve declaration of state of emergency

Other Powers:

  • • Elect the President of the Republic
  • • Approve declarations of war
  • • Grant amnesty

🏛️ House of Federation (HoF)

A. Composition (Article 61)

Membership Basis:

  • • Composed of representatives of Nations, Nationalities, and Peoples
  • • Each Nation, Nationality, and People represented by at least one member
  • • Additional one representative per one million population

Method of Election:

  • • Members are elected by the State Councils (Regional Councils)
  • • State Councils may provide for direct elections, but this option has never been exercised

Term: Members serve according to the term of the State Council that elected them.

B. Powers and Functions

Constitutional Interpretation (Article 83):

  • • The House of Federation has the power to interpret the Constitution
  • • Final authority on constitutional disputes

Dispute Resolution:

  • • Decide disputes between states
  • • Decide disputes between federal and state governments

Rights of Nations and Nationalities:

  • • Decide on issues related to self-determination
  • • Determine the right of a Nation/Nationality to secede

Constitutional Amendment:

  • • Participates in constitutional amendment process (Articles 104-105)

Fiscal and Budgetary Role:

  • • Determine formula for revenue sharing
  • • Approve federal subsidies to states

Other Powers:

  • • Order federal intervention if a state endangers constitutional order
  • • Establish the Council of Constitutional Inquiry

C. Council of Constitutional Inquiry (CCI)

Composition:

  • • Established by the House of Federation
  • • Composed of legal experts and judicial representatives

Role:

  • • Investigates constitutional disputes
  • • Submits its recommendations to the House of Federation
  • • House of Federation makes final decision

👔 The Executive Branch

A. The Prime Minister

Position and Election:

  • • Head of Government
  • • Elected from among members of the House of Peoples' Representatives
  • • Chosen by the party in power following elections

Powers and Functions:

Category Powers
Executive Powers Chief Executive; chairs Council of Ministers; oversees implementation of laws
Appointment Powers Nominates ministers; appoints high-level officials (with HPR approval)
Military Powers Commander-in-Chief of armed forces
Foreign Affairs Directs foreign policy; represents Ethiopia internationally
Emergency Powers Submits state of emergency declaration to HPR
⚠️ Accountability:
  • • The Council of Ministers is accountable to the Prime Minister
  • • The Council of Ministers is responsible to the House of Peoples' Representatives

B. The Council of Ministers (Article 76-77)

Composition:

  • ✔️ Prime Minister
  • ✔️ Deputy Prime Minister
  • ✔️ Ministers
  • ✔️ Other members as determined by law

Powers and Functions:

Area Functions
Policy Implementation Ensure implementation of laws and policies
Administration Direct and coordinate government ministries and departments
Legislative Role Draft laws and submit to HPR; issue regulations
Budget Prepare and submit federal budget
Foreign Affairs Conduct foreign relations; negotiate treaties
National Security Ensure national security; maintain public order
Emergency Declare state of emergency (subject to HPR approval)

C. The President

Position:

  • • Head of State
  • • Elected by the House of Peoples' Representatives
  • • Term: Six years (renewable once, maximum 12 years)

Powers (largely ceremonial/honorary):

  • • Opens joint sessions of Parliament
  • • Appoints ambassadors (upon recommendation)
  • • Receives foreign diplomats
  • • Awards medals and honors
  • • Grants pardons (upon recommendation)

Qualifications:

  • • Ethiopian citizen
  • • Of good character and reputation

⚖️ The Judiciary

A. Judicial Independence

Constitutional Guarantees:

Guarantee Description
Institutional Independence Courts free from interference by other branches
Decisional Independence Judges decide cases solely by the law, without influence
Personal Independence Security of tenure; cannot be removed except for cause
Financial Independence Judiciary budget protected

Purpose of Independence:

  • • Ensure judges decide cases solely based on law and facts
  • • Protect against political pressure
  • • Maintain impartiality
  • • Uphold rule of law

B. Appointment of Judges

Federal Courts:

  • • Judges appointed by the Council of Ministers
  • • Upon recommendation by the Federal Judicial Administration Council

Qualifications:

  • • Legal education and professional experience
  • • Good character and reputation
  • • Demonstrated competence

C. Jurisdiction

Federal Supreme Court:

  • • Highest federal court
  • • Hears appeals from lower federal courts
  • • Has cassation power (review of fundamental errors of law)

Federal High Courts:

  • • Original jurisdiction over serious federal cases
  • • Appellate jurisdiction over First Instance Courts

Federal First Instance Courts:

  • • Trial courts for federal matters

State Courts:

  • • Have jurisdiction over state matters
  • • Parallel structure (Supreme, High, First Instance)

📜 Constitutional Amendment & Interpretation (Articles 104-105)

⚙️ Two-Tier Amendment Process

The FDRE Constitution establishes different procedures for amending different categories of provisions.

🅰️ A. Amendment of Fundamental Principles (Article 105)

Provisions Requiring Stricter Procedure:

  • Chapter One (General Provisions)
  • Chapter Two (Fundamental Principles)
  • Chapter Six (Structure and Powers of Organs of Government)
  • Article 104 (itself, which governs amendments)

Procedure:

Step Requirement
1. Initiation Proposal by either House or State Councils
2. HPR Consideration Must be supported by 2/3 majority vote of House of Peoples' Representatives
3. State Council Approval Must be approved by 2/3 majority of the State Councils of member states

🅱️ B. Amendment of Other Provisions (Article 104)

Procedure for non-entrenched provisions:

Step Requirement
1. Initiation Proposal by either House or State Councils
2. HPR Consideration Must be supported by 2/3 majority vote of House of Peoples' Representatives
3. HoF Consideration Must be approved by 2/3 majority vote of House of Federation
💡 Key Points to Remember:
  • The Constitution cannot be repealed, only amended
  • No single body can amend the Constitution alone
  • Federal and state governments both participate in the process

⚖️ 2. Council of Constitutional Inquiry (CCI)

🏛️ Establishment and Composition

The Council of Constitutional Inquiry is established by the House of Federation to assist in constitutional interpretation.

Composition:

  • Legal experts
  • Judicial representatives
  • Appointed by the House of Federation

🎯 Functions and Role

Investigative Role:

  • Investigates constitutional disputes referred to it
  • Examines whether laws, decisions, or practices conform to the Constitution

Advisory Role:

  • Submits its recommendations to the House of Federation
  • Provides expert legal analysis on constitutional questions

Referral:

When a constitutional issue arises in court proceedings, the court may refer it to the CCI.

🏢 House of Federation's Interpretive Role

Final Authority: Article 83 grants the House of Federation the power to interpret the Constitution.

Scope of Interpretive Power:

Area Application
Constitutional Disputes Final authority on all constitutional questions
Federal-State Conflicts Resolves disputes between federal and state governments
Inter-State Conflicts Resolves disputes between states
Rights of Nations/Nationalities Determines issues related to self-determination and rights
Constitutionality of Laws Decides whether laws conform to the Constitution

🔄 Process Flow

  1. Constitutional question arises (in court, government, or public discourse)
  2. Matter may be referred to CCI for investigation
  3. CCI submits recommendation to House of Federation
  4. House of Federation makes final and binding decision
🔍 Distinction from Judicial Review:
  • Unlike in some countries where courts conduct constitutional review, in Ethiopia this power rests with the House of Federation (political body)
  • Courts interpret and apply laws but cannot finally determine constitutionality

🌐 Division of Powers in Federal System

📌 Overview of Power Distribution

The FDRE Constitution divides powers between the Federal Government and Regional States based on the principle of residual powers.

📊 Three Categories of Powers

Category Description Constitutional Basis
Exclusive Federal Powers Matters assigned solely to Federal Government Article 51
Concurrent Powers Matters on which both federal and state governments may legislate Article 52
Residual/State Powers Powers not expressly given to federal government or concurrent Reserved to state

🛡️ Exclusive Federal Powers (Article 51)

Key Federal Powers:

  • National Defense and Security:
    • National defense, federal police, and security forces
    • Declare war and state of emergency
  • Foreign Affairs:
    • Foreign policy and diplomatic relations
    • Negotiate and ratify international agreements
    • Represent Ethiopia internationally
  • Economic and Financial Matters:
    • Print currency and regulate money supply
    • Determine foreign exchange rates
    • Regulate foreign trade and inter-state commerce
    • Levy and collect federal taxes and duties
    • Prepare and administer federal budget
  • Infrastructure and Transportation:
    • Air, rail, and water transportation (inter-state and international)
    • Postal and telecommunications services
    • Major roads linking states
  • Natural Resources:
    • Regulate utilization of land and natural resources (shared with states)
    • Develop and administer water resources crossing state boundaries
  • Citizenship and Immigration:
    • Nationality and immigration matters
    • Passports and entry/exit permits
  • Standards and Measurements:
    • Establish and enforce national standards
    • Weights and measures
    • Calendar and time
  • Criminal Law and Procedure:
    • Enact penal code (may be adapted by states)
    • Establish federal courts

🤝 Concurrent Powers

Concurrent powers are those exercised by both federal and state governments on the same subject matter.

Areas of Concurrent Jurisdiction:

Area Federal Role State Role
Taxation Federal taxes State taxes (non-conflicting)
Law Enforcement Federal crimes State crimes
Education Higher education policy Primary/secondary education
Health National health policy Health service delivery
Land and Natural Resources Regulatory framework Administration and use
Social Services Policy guidelines Implementation
⚖️ Supremacy Clause in Concurrent Areas:

Where federal and state laws conflict on concurrent matters, federal law prevails (Article 9 supremacy).

🏛️ Residual/State Powers (Article 52)

Residual powers are those not expressly assigned to the Federal Government or not designated as concurrent. These powers are reserved to the States.

State Powers:

  • Legislative Powers:
    • Enact state constitution and laws
    • Establish state administrative organs
  • Administrative Powers:
    • Administer land and natural resources within the state (consistent with federal law)
    • Establish state police force
    • Implement state economic and social development programs
    • Provide primary and secondary education
    • Deliver health services
  • Fiscal Powers:
    • Levy and collect state taxes
    • Prepare and administer state budget
    • Receive federal subsidies
  • Judicial Powers:
    • Establish state courts
    • Administer justice on state matters
  • Cultural and Linguistic Powers:
    • Determine working language of the state
    • Promote and develop state culture and language

🔄 Devolution vs. Delegation

🅰️ A. Devolution of Power

The statutory transfer of powers from the central government to regional or local governments, creating autonomous decision-making authority.

Characteristics:

  • Power is permanently transferred
  • Recipient exercises power in its own right
  • Recipient is not subordinate in that area
  • Constitutionally protected
  • Cannot be unilaterally withdrawn

Examples in FDRE Constitution:

  • States have constitutional power over their internal affairs
  • States determine their working language
  • States establish their own administrative structures

🅱️ B. Delegation of Power

The temporary assignment of functions by one authority to another, where the delegating authority retains ultimate responsibility.

Characteristics:

  • Power is temporarily assigned
  • Recipient acts on behalf of delegator
  • Recipient is accountable to delegator
  • Can be withdrawn at any time
  • Not constitutionally protected

Examples:

  • Federal government delegating certain functions to state administrations
  • Executive agencies exercising delegated legislative authority (regulations)

⚖️ Comparison Table

Aspect Devolution Delegation
Nature Permanent transfer Temporary assignment
Legal Basis Constitutional Statutory/administrative
Authority Exercised in own right Exercised on behalf of delegator
Accountability To own constituents To delegating authority
Withdrawal Requires constitutional amendment Can be unilaterally withdrawn
Protection Constitutionally protected Not constitutionally protected

🚨 State of Emergency & Special Powers

📜 Constitutional Framework (Article 93)

Purpose of Emergency Provisions:

To enable the government to respond effectively to exceptional circumstances threatening the nation, while maintaining constitutional governance to the extent possible.

Grounds for Declaration:

Article 93 allows declaration of state of emergency when:

  • External invasion
  • Internal instability threatening constitutional order
  • Natural disaster
  • Epidemic or other extreme circumstances endangering public order

📋 Declaration Procedures

A. Initial Declaration:

Step Actor Action
1. Declaration Council of Ministers Formally declares state of emergency
2. Submission Council of Ministers Submits declaration to House of Peoples' Representatives

B. Approval Process:

Step Actor Requirement
1. Initial Approval House of Peoples' Representatives Must approve declaration within 48 hours
2. If House not in session House of Peoples' Representatives Must convene within 48 hours to consider declaration
3. Effect of Non-approval - Declaration automatically terminated

⏳ Duration and Renewal

Initial Duration:

  • State of emergency remains in effect for the period specified in the declaration
  • Maximum initial period: Six months

Renewal:

Step Requirement
1. Request for Extension Council of Ministers submits request to HPR
2. Approval Requires two-thirds majority vote of House of Peoples' Representatives
3. Extension Period Each extension may not exceed four months

🛑 Limitations on Emergency Powers

A. Non-Derogable Rights:

Even during a state of emergency, certain fundamental rights cannot be suspended:

Article Right
Article 1 Nomenclature of the State (cannot be changed)
Article 14 Right to life
Article 15 Protection of right to life
Article 18 Prohibition against inhuman treatment
Article 25 Right to equality
Article 39(5) Rights of Nations, Nationalities and Peoples (cannot be suspended)

B. Proportionality:

  • Measures taken during emergency must be proportional to the threat
  • Cannot exceed what is strictly required by the situation

C. Judicial Review:

  • Courts retain jurisdiction to examine whether emergency measures comply with the Constitution
  • Individuals may challenge the legality of their detention

D. Parliamentary Oversight:

  • HPR monitors implementation of emergency measures
  • Can revoke or modify the declaration at any time

E. International Obligations:

  • Emergency measures must comply with Ethiopia's international human rights obligations
  • Must notify relevant international bodies of derogation
